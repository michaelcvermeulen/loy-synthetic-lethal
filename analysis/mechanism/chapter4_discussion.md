## 4.4 Discussion

### 4.4.1 Overview of principal findings

This chapter evaluated whether loss of the Y chromosome creates actionable genetic or pharmacological vulnerabilities in male cancer cells, both through loss of Y-linked paralog buffering and through broader dependencies shaped by the Y-depleted cellular state. Combining pan-cancer dependency and drug-sensitivity compendia with genome-wide Brunello CRISPR–Cas9 screens in the isogenic Y-depleted HT1080 and A549 models from Chapter 3 revealed a clear division between universal and context-restricted effects. Across heterogeneous cell-line panels and three-dimensional malignant organoid cohorts, synthetic lethality with Y loss was restricted to the four ancestral X–Y gametolog pairs *DDX3X*–*DDX3Y*, *EIF1AX*–*EIF1AY*, *RPS4X*–*RPS4Y1* and *ZFX*–*ZFY*, whereas lineage- and driver-stratified searches were statistically underpowered to resolve moderate non-gametolog effects. In matched isogenic screens, where Y-depleted and control populations share a parental genome, HT1080 and A549 recovered *DDX3X* and *EIF1AX*, respectively, in accordance with their parental baseline dependency profiles and guide dropout kinetics, while nominating background-specific non-gametolog candidates in SAGA/Mediator chromatin coactivation, SREBP-regulated lipid and selenoprotein homeostasis, and X-linked mitochondrial import. Finally, developing a reciprocal two-colour eGFP/mCherry competitive growth assay quantified by spectral flow cytometry provided preliminary evidence that targeted *DDX3X* and *TADA2B* disruption reduced the relative fitness of Y-depleted HT1080 cells over the initial four-day competition window; independent replication remains pending.

### 4.4.2 Evolutionary and functional rules of X–Y paralog buffering

The restriction of pan-cancer LOY dependencies in DepMap to *DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, *RPS4X* and *ZFX* reflects both the evolutionary history of the human sex chromosomes and the functional architecture of pooled proliferation screens. Mammalian Y chromosomes have selectively retained a small core of ubiquitously expressed, dosage-sensitive ancestral X–Y gene pairs ([Bellott et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13206)), and consistent with [Shohat et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.276488.121), dependency on each of these four X-linked genes tracked expression of its cognate Y-linked partner even in a graded manner among Y-retaining lines. In a negative-selection viability screen, an X–Y pair scores broadly only when (i) the shared activity of the pair is widely essential for cell division and survival and (ii) the Y-linked paralog provides sufficient somatic activity to buffer X-gene loss in Y-retaining cells. *RPS4X*, *EIF1AX*, *DDX3X* and, in a subset of models, *ZFX* satisfy both conditions across Y-null lines without requiring steady-state transcriptional upregulation of the remaining X allele, consistent with observations in clear cell renal cell carcinoma models ([Waterhölter et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.08.757570)). Recent mechanistic studies explain how paralog buffering can coexist with unchanged steady-state mRNA levels, demonstrating that *DDX3X* and *DDX3Y* buffer combined dosage through negative post-transcriptional cross-regulation of mRNA stability and proteasome-sensitive protein turnover ([Rengarajan et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.279707.124); [Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2026.1644807)). Consequently, a Y-depleted cell can maintain near-normal baseline DDX3 protein abundance while losing its functional reserve when *DDX3X* is subsequently perturbed. By contrast, the remaining X–Y gametolog pairs fail at least one of the two conditions. Although the deubiquitinase *USP9X* is broadly essential across male models, *USP9Y* has restricted somatic expression and fails to buffer *USP9X* knockout in Y-retaining cells. Conversely, the histone demethylases *KDM5C* and *KDM6A*/*UTX* and the corepressor *TBL1X* are selectively essential in very few cell lines and instead act as X-inactivation-escaping tumour suppressors in lineages such as renal and bladder carcinoma ([Dunford et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.3701); [Abdel-Hafiz et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06234-x)), while *PRKX*, *TXLNG* and *NLGN4X* are non-essential for in vitro proliferation. Even among the four pan-cancer hits, *ZFX* exhibited the weakest pan-cancer shift and did not replicate in three-dimensional organoid cohorts or in our HT1080 and A549 screens. This weaker penetrance is explained by the fact that ZFX and ZFY belong to a three-member C2H2 zinc-finger family alongside X-linked ZNF711; all three factors co-occupy the same active CpG-island promoters genome-wide, and ZNF711 functionally buffers ZFX loss in ZFY-null cells ([Ni et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaa384)).

Beyond these four paralog pairs, two factors explain why context-stratified genetic and pharmacological comparisons across DepMap, CTRPv2, GDSC1/2 and PRISM yielded almost no additional hits, and why reported *KDM5D*-associated ATR-inhibitor sensitivity in prostate cancer ([Komura et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI96769)) could not be evaluated given the scarcity of LOY prostate lines. First, pooled monoculture screens measure only cell-autonomous proliferation and cannot detect microenvironmental or immunological consequences of Y loss, such as the T-cell exhaustion and adaptive immune evasion that accelerate LOY tumour growth in vivo without altering in vitro division rates ([Abdel-Hafiz et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06234-x)). Second, our power simulations showed that partitioning cell-line panels by lineage and driver mutation leaves sample sizes too small relative to inter-tumoural genetic background variance to detect moderate synthetic lethalities. Moving to matched isogenic screens overcame this background variance while illustrating the technical determinants of paralog recovery (**Fig** 4.3, **Fig** 4.4b, **Fig** 4.8b). In HT1080, where parental cells are unusually tolerant of *DDX3X* loss at baseline, Y depletion unmasked *DDX3X* as the strongest genome-wide sensitizer, consistent with ribosome-profiling and lymphoma studies showing that DDX3Y substitutes for DDX3X to translate mRNAs with structured, GC-rich $5'$ leaders ([Venkataramanan et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1261/rna.078926.121); [Gong et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2021.07.041)) and with targeted *DDX3X*/*DDX3Y* co-depletion and transgene-rescue experiments in HT1080 and KNS-42 ([Köferle et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2022.110636)). Parental A549, by contrast, expresses very low baseline Y-linked transcript levels (Chapter 3) and is already strongly *DDX3X*-dependent in DepMap, leaving little dynamic range for further *DDX3X* sensitization while allowing *EIF1AX* to score at endpoint, although statistical power in A549 was constrained by having only two `P4` libraries. Conversely, *EIF1AX* and *RPS4X* failed to score in HT1080 because sgRNAs targeting such core housekeeping genes depleted heavily during pre-D0 antibiotic selection in both genotypes, compressing endpoint counts to the sequencing floor. Moreover, Y-depleted HT1080 cells retained a distal Yp fragment expressing *RPS4Y1* (Chapter 3), representing a partial-LOY state at the *RPS4Y1* locus; because viable *RPS4X* knockouts cannot be recovered in male cells and *RPS4Y1* deletion triggers compensatory upregulation of RPS4X protein ([Reddy et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms26136213)), both residual RPS4Y1 synthesis and feedback stabilization of RPS4X likely further blunted any differential *RPS4X* readout. Capturing paralog synthetic lethality for core essential genes in pooled screens therefore requires sampling immediately after transduction or employing inducible Cas9/CRISPRi systems that preserve guide representation through selection.

### 4.4.3 Chromatin coactivator, lipid, and mitochondrial adaptations beyond paralog buffering

The non-gametolog candidates suggest distinct regulatory dependencies in HT1080 and A549. In HT1080, multiple SAGA Core/HAT subunits grouped with Mediator and other chromatin regulators, whereas the DUB-associated *ATXN7L3* showed a weaker co-dependency relationship (**Fig** 4.3d, **Fig** 4.4c; **Supp Fig** 4.8). Disruption of SAGA Core subunits can destabilize the scaffold and trigger UBR5-mediated degradation of orphaned KAT2A ([Batty et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71613-7)), providing a mechanistic precedent for concordant effects across several subunits. In A549, the strongest candidates supported by both endpoint and D0-adjusted analyses were *L3MBTL2* and *UBR5*. Because UBR5 loss can stabilize orphaned KAT2A, its nomination does not imply the same biochemical mechanism as SAGA disruption. PRC1.6-mediated transcriptional restraint and UBR5-dependent control of MYC and damaged chromatin offer alternative hypotheses for this background ([Stielow et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1007193); [Qiao et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-19-1647); [Sanchez et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1610735113)). Their connection to loss of *KDM5D*, which can restrain replication stress through H3K4me3 regulation, remains to be tested ([Li et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-15-0906); [Komura et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI96769)).

Chapter 3 molecular profiles constrain, but do not resolve, the HT1080 mechanism. The nominated SAGA genes and *MED12* did not show altered baseline mRNA expression or promoter accessibility (**Supp Fig** 4.11), so their dependency cannot be explained simply by transcriptional silencing of the hit genes. Reduced MYC target expression also occurred in A549 without SAGA scoring there. A broader enhancer-maintenance model is plausible: UTX/UTY can support accessibility and acetylation through demethylase-independent functions, while *KMT2D*, *CREBBP* and Mediator participate in overlapping enhancer networks ([Gozdecka et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-018-0114-z); [Akiyama et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1242/dev.205328); [Lee et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.01503); [Freimer et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08314-y)). Loss of UTY could increase reliance on these remaining coactivators, but the relevant UTY evidence comes from other cell types and whole-Y depletion removes several regulators simultaneously. Targeted gene loss and complementation are required to connect this model to the screen phenotype.

The external screens reinforce this qualification. SAGA, *MED12*, *CREBBP* and *SREBF1* effects recurred after non-LOY perturbations, including *KEAP1* mutation and 13q loss, while SAGA gene sets scored in opposite directions across backgrounds (**Supp Fig** 4.15; [Desjardins et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117961); [Feng et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9106303/)). SAGA dependency is therefore neither uniform nor specific to Y loss. The value of the HT1080 result is the convergence of several candidate subunits and complementary analyses within a defined model.

The *SREBF1/2*, *ACSL4* and *SCLY* candidates similarly support a lipid/redox-regulatory hypothesis without establishing a single downstream mechanism. Canonical SREBP target sets were unchanged at baseline, and many upstream processing factors and downstream sterol enzymes did not score (**Supp Fig** 4.11a). Serum lipids could buffer individual biosynthetic reactions, or SREBF1/2 could be required through broader membrane, acetyl-CoA or stress-response functions. The immune-cell response to partial *Kdm5d* depletion reported by [Li et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.23.740424) concerns a different cell type and cannot establish the cancer-cell mechanism. Moreover, ACSL4 loss normally protects against canonical ferroptosis ([Doll et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.2239)), so its nomination alongside the selenium-recycling enzyme SCLY does not by itself demonstrate ferroptosis hypersensitivity. Lipid supplementation and direct redox measurements would help distinguish these explanations.

Finally, the mitochondrial resistor signal describes relative fitness during outgrowth rather than a gain of mitochondrial function (**Supp Fig** 4.10a,b). Lower baseline expression of mitochondrial programs could accompany a reduced requirement for respiratory-chain and mitochondrial-translation genes (**Supp Fig** 4.11a). The sensitizer *TIMM8A* may instead reflect a more specific requirement for mitochondrial import or complex-IV assembly ([Kang et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.48828)). These contrasting directions caution against treating all mitochondrial candidates as one phenotype and prioritize functional tests over a single pathway-wide explanation.

### 4.4.4 Methodological lessons, study limitations, and causal validation priorities

Our screening and validation workflow highlighted several methodological principles for isogenic CRISPR studies in engineered chromosome-loss models. First, whereas splitting a single transduced pool at `D0` into parallel outgrowth cultures (`R1` and `R2`) is standard practice in genome-wide screens, each genotype in HT1080 and A549 derived from a single established population from Chapter 3; the outgrowth replicates therefore measure technical and culture variation after the `D0` split rather than independent Y-elimination events, and cannot by themselves separate the effects of Y loss from accompanying subclonal copy-number changes such as the distal 13q gain and chromosome 4 loss in HT1080. Second, our cross-screen quality-control audit showed that severe PCR jackpotting in the `D0` sequencing libraries, which also recurred in external screens (**Supp Fig** 4.16), propagates baseline distortion into D0-adjusted scores. Pairing primary endpoint-only `drugZ` analysis with guide-masked, D0-adjusted `IsogenicZ` sensitivity analysis mitigated this trade-off and confirmed that the core HT1080 sensitizers and pathway themes were supported under both frameworks (**Supp Fig** 4.7b).

To validate individual candidates outside a pooled screen, our reciprocal two-colour eGFP/mCherry competitive growth assay combined co-culture in a shared microenvironment, within-well `D0` normalization, and dye-swap reporter orientations with spectral unmixing on the `BD FACSDiscover A8` to isolate genotype-specific fitness effects from fluorophore-intrinsic growth offsets (**Fig** 4.5, **Fig** 4.6). While the interim `D0`–`D4` measurements confirmed that *DDX3X* knockout across matched six-clone pools and *TADA2B* knockout in heterogeneous populations selectively abolished the baseline proliferative advantage of Y-depleted HT1080 cells in both reporter orientations, several experimental steps are required to complete validation. First, completing the full `D8`–`D28` longitudinal competition time course across independent replicate nucleofections for *DDX3X*, *TADA2B*, *TAF6L*, *SREBF1*, *SREBF2*, *KIF2A*, *MED12* and *TIMM8A* is necessary to establish multi-passage reproducibility. Second, pre-sorting $\text{GFP}^+$ and $\text{mCherry}^+$ sublines to purity prior to RNP delivery and testing multiple independently derived Y-depleted clones or mosaic pools will eliminate the reporter-negative subpopulation and subline divergence that confounded the heterogeneous *TADA2B* and *SREBF1* pools. Third, editing efficiency and protein depletion must be quantified by amplicon sequencing (CRISPResso2) and Western blotting using antibodies that recognize epitopes downstream of the edited exon, or by targeted proteomics, to rule out retention of truncated protein via alternative translation initiation ([Xu et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcell.2026.1644807)). Finally, establishing causal attribution to specific Y-linked genes will require testing both whether lentiviral *DDX3Y* or *UTY* cDNA complementation rescues the competitive fitness penalty of *DDX3X* and *TADA2B*/*TAF6L* knockouts in Y-depleted cells and whether selective *DDX3Y* or *UTY* knockout in Y-retaining parental cells is sufficient to phenocopy those dependencies.

Translation also requires target selectivity and an acceptable therapeutic window. DDX3X/DDX3Y structural similarity complicates selective inhibition, while age-related mosaic Y loss raises the possibility of toxicity in non-malignant Y-deficient cells. Chapter 5 examines these translational constraints. Here, the combined public-data analyses, isogenic screens and interim competition assays prioritize vulnerabilities for the independent and gene-specific validation outlined above.
