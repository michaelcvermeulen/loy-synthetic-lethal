## 3.4 Discussion

### 3.4.1 Overview of principal findings

The goal of this chapter was to establish experimental models of Y-chromosome loss and investigate its molecular consequences in cancer cells, providing a foundation for testing synthetic lethal vulnerabilities associated with Y loss in Chapter 4. Isolating the direct effects of Y loss from genetic heterogeneity across tumours, cancer types and cell lines requires matched Y-retaining and Y-depleted populations derived from the same parental background, paired with genome-wide characterization to define their retained gene content and secondary karyotypic changes. By designing a repeat-targeting CRISPR–Cas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) strategy from the complete T2T-Y assembly, we achieved substantial Y-chromosome depletion in pooled populations of HT1080, A549 and DU145 cells without requiring single-cell cloning. Integrating ddPCR, lpWGS and RNA-seq revealed that while A549 underwent near-complete loss across both chromosome arms, HT1080 and especially DU145 selectively retained distal Yp sequences despite complete euchromatic Yq elimination. Transcriptional profiling across the three backgrounds demonstrated that autosomal gene-level changes were largely lineage-specific, whereas reduced interferon, inflammatory and rRNA-processing gene-set expression recurred across models. Finally, ATAC-seq and targeted UTY and KDM5D rescue experiments in HT1080 linked Y depletion to widespread chromatin closing at downregulated and Polycomb-associated promoters and showed that UTY rescue partially reversed the established LOY transcriptional signature.

### 3.4.2 Engineering and genomic characterization of pooled Y-depleted models

Targeting repetitive Y-linked arrays enabled efficient depletion of Y-chromosomal DNA across multiple cancer backgrounds while highlighting how guide identity, combinatorial cleavage and regional retention shape the outcome of chromosome engineering. Combining centromeric (CP) and Yq12 heterochromatic (Q53) guides was particularly effective in A549, where individual guides reduced Y-linked DNA dosage by only 5–14% whereas combined CP + Q53 delivery reduced AMELY and DDX3Y dosage by 91–94% after a single nucleofection and by ~99% after two successive nucleofections. At the same time, our single-guide screen in HT1080 showed that predicted copy-number abundance alone does not guarantee cleavage efficiency. Using the complete HG002 T2T-Y assembly ([Rhie et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06457-y)), our unbiased k-mer search independently recovered the DYZ3 centromeric guide used within a three-guide mixture by [Celli et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13578-024-01198-5) as CN, alongside a second opposite-strand DYZ3 guide (CP) and the DYZ1/DYZ18 heterochromatic guide Q53. Despite similar target-site counts and overlapping spatial distributions across the DYZ3 array, CP reduced Y-linked transcript abundance by 84–94% in HT1080 whereas CN produced minimal reduction, underscoring the importance of empirically testing individual repeat-targeting guides before pooling. We also showed in DU145 that transient HDAC inhibition with panobinostat prior to RNP delivery ([Liu et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkz1136)) enhanced single-round Y depletion to match two drug-free nucleofections without inducing additional off-target autosomal copy-number alterations in lpWGS, offering a viable option to streamline single-round editing when transient chromatin-drug exposure is acceptable.

A key methodological feature of our workflow was the use of transient RNP nucleofection followed by expansion of pooled populations rather than single-cell clones. Earlier Y-depletion studies relied on plasmid or lentiviral Cas9 delivery and clonal isolation ([Adikusuma et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2017.05.021); [Zuo et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-017-1354-4); [Celli et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13578-024-01198-5)), and recent cancer LOY preprints profiled either two RNP-derived YAPC clones against parental cells ([Dorsch et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.756936)) or five Y-negative versus five Y-retaining 786-O clones isolated after lentiviral delivery ([Waterhölter et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.30.702819)). Transient RNP delivery restricts nuclease activity to a brief window, avoiding the continual cutting, heightened risk of cumulative off-target cleavage and chronic DNA-damage responses that can arise when repetitive guides are expressed from integrated plasmid or lentiviral constructs and confound transcriptomic readouts. In parallel, expanding edited cells as bulk pools avoids fixing clone-specific passenger mutations and epigenetic drift, which is particularly advantageous in genetically unstable cancer cell lines. Nevertheless, pooled populations can still undergo subclonal selection during post-editing recovery, and our genome-wide lpWGS profiles revealed accompanying copy-number shifts in HT1080 (distal 13q gain and chromosome 4 loss) and DU145 (20p loss and chromosome X and 15q gains). Notably, the 20p copy-number reduction in DU145 recurred across six independent Y-targeted editing conditions relative to sgCTRL. Determining whether such recurrent autosomal alterations reflect sequence-specific off-target cleavage in a given cell line, expansion of a pre-existing subclone, or selective pressure favouring specific chromosomal gains or losses to compensate for Y depletion will require profiling multiple independently derived pools alongside parental sublines and mapping off-target cleavage directly.

Integrating single-locus ddPCR with chromosome-wide lpWGS and RNA-seq also demonstrated that regional Yp retention is a recurring outcome of repeat-targeted Y cleavage. Whereas A549 lost the entire Y chromosome across both arms, HT1080 retained ~26% of RPS4Y1 and ZFY expression in distal Yp, and DU145 retained a discrete distal Yp segment (~2.46–4.80 Mb) encompassing RPS4Y1 and ZFY at 46–54% of sgCTRL expression despite 96–98% loss at AMELY (~6.5 Mb) and across euchromatic Yq. Similar preferential retention of short-arm sequences after long-arm repeat targeting was observed in mouse embryonic stem cells ([Adikusuma et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2017.05.021)). This finding demonstrates that standard single-locus AMELY/AMELX ddPCR cannot by itself distinguish whole-Y loss from partial Y truncation when AMELY lies proximal to a retained distal Yp fragment. Comparing the copy-number ploidy of PAR1 and the retained distal male-specific Yp segment, combined with metaphase FISH or long-read sequencing across breakpoint junctions, will be informative to determine whether the entire distal tip (PAR1 through ZFY) persists as a centric fragment or is stabilized by translocation onto an autosome or the X chromosome.

### 3.4.3 Lineage-specific and recurring transcriptional consequences of Y depletion

Across HT1080, A549 and DU145, Y depletion consistently reduced summed PAR1 expression by 23–33% without triggering compensatory transcriptional upregulation of the X-linked paralogs of lost male-specific Y genes. This absence of transcriptional compensation agrees with observations in constitutional sex-chromosome aneuploidies ([Blanton et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2024.100628)) and means that losing Y-linked genes directly reduces combined X + Y paralog dosage at baseline, whereas the partial retention of distal Yp genes (RPS4Y1 and ZFY) in HT1080 and DU145 distinguishes partially retained Yp paralogs from fully depleted Yq paralogs (such as DDX3Y and EIF1AY) when interpreting paralog dependencies in Chapter 4. Outside the sex chromosomes, however, gene-by-gene differential expression showed virtually no correlation across the three cell lines ($\rho = -0.06$ to $0.11$), and near-complete whole-Y loss in A549 produced far fewer individually significant autosomal genes than partial Y depletion in HT1080 or DU145. A similarly modest global transcriptional separation with 40 differentially expressed autosomal genes was recently reported across Y-negative and Y-retaining 786-O renal carcinoma clones ([Waterhölter et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.01.30.702819)). These observations indicate that the autosomal transcriptional response to Y loss is strongly shaped by cell lineage and baseline regulatory state rather than governed by a single universal gene-level signature.

At the pathway level, coordinated downregulation of interferon-alpha, interferon-gamma and inflammatory response signatures, together with rRNA processing, represented the clearest recurring feature across our three engineered models, led by shared reductions in transcripts such as BST2 and MX1. Lower interferon-alpha response expression also recurred across multiple external comparisons, including naturally Y-negative HT1080, A549 and J82 cells, mouse MB49 tumours, constitutional 45,X models and UTY-knockout human embryonic stem cells, as well as acute RPTEC Yq-loss scRNA-seq profiles. Detecting dampened basal interferon and inflammatory transcription in pure cancer-cell cultures indicates that Y loss can alter cell-intrinsic immune-signalling states independently of infiltrating leukocytes, potentially contributing to the T-cell exhaustion and immune-evasion phenotypes observed in LOY tumours in vivo ([Abdel-Hafiz et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06234-x); [Chen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08671-2)). Because bulk RNA-seq measures steady-state transcript abundance rather than signalling capacity, orthogonal validation of candidate immune transcripts and proteins (BST2, MHC class I/$\beta_2$-microglobulin and chemokines) by RT-qPCR, flow cytometry and cytokine profiling before and after interferon stimulation is needed to test whether reduced basal expression reflects blunted JAK–STAT responsiveness.

Alongside shared immune dampening, our models exhibited lineage-dependent remodelling of epithelial, ECM and developmental programs. A reference signature of genes downregulated after CDH1 knockdown ([Onder et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-07-2938)) was negatively enriched across all three lines and in half of the 18 external Y-loss contrasts, and the basement-membrane peroxidative cross-linking enzyme PXDN ([Bhave et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.1038)) was reduced across all three models. In DU145, copy-number-stable differential expression revealed coordinated suppression of epithelial identity markers (TACSTD2, CLDN7, ST14, AP1M2, OVOL1 and GRHL2) alongside elevated basement-membrane collagen transcripts (COL4A1, COL4A2 and COL12A1) and opposite shifts in ANXA8 and ANXA1 ([Mattisson et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12864-024-10168-7)), without full induction of canonical EMT transcription factors. In A549, proliferation and oxidative-phosphorylation signatures were reduced without a corresponding defect in basal growth rate, paralleling reports that Y-negative A549 and pancreatic cancer models display context-dependent clonogenic, invasive and metastatic phenotypes rather than uniform changes in unperturbed proliferation ([Schlüter et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.02.729627); [Dorsch et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.06.756936); [Li et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1158/0008-5472.CAN-15-0906)). Together, these recurring adhesion and matrix alterations suggest that Y loss may prime epithelial cancer cells for altered ECM remodelling, invasion and metastasis, a hypothesis that warrants direct functional testing in three-dimensional invasion and in vivo dissemination assays.

Our comparison with 16 public chromosome- and arm-loss contrasts ([Chunduri et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25288-x); [Bosco et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.03.029); [Girish et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg4521); [Feng et al., 2025, preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.25.650684); [Dahiya et al., 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.737056)) further contextualized which pathway shifts may reflect general responses to chromosome loss versus Y-specific regulation. Reduced ribosomal-protein expression occurred across most autosomal monosomies and arm losses, and acute Yq and 3p loss in RPTECs shared p53 activation and E2F suppression while diverging in type I interferon signalling (downregulated after Yq loss but upregulated after 3p loss), although the acute Yq-loss group included only 194 RNA-classified cells. Moreover, within the same RPTEC 3p-loss clones, interferon-alpha and E2F signatures reversed direction between day 18 and day 90 of culture. Thus, pathway states measured in established chromosome-loss populations reflect both the identity of the lost chromosome and temporal adaptation during expansion.

### 3.4.4 Chromatin accessibility and partial transcriptional rescue by UTY

In HT1080, integrating ATAC-seq with RNA-seq demonstrated that Y depletion is accompanied by a sevenfold skew toward chromatin closing across consensus regulatory regions, and that downregulated genes are more than 30-fold more likely than non-differentially expressed genes to lose promoter accessibility. Furthermore, continuous TSS insertion coverage was reduced by 21% even across downregulated promoters that did not pass peak-level significance thresholds, indicating that promoter compaction is a broad correlate of gene repression in Y-depleted HT1080 cells. Promoters undergoing coordinated RNA and accessibility loss, as well as closing promoters genome-wide, were significantly enriched for reference PRC2 and H3K27me3 target signatures even after matching for baseline accessibility, GC content and CpG density. At the same time, subset-specific derepression of high-CpG H3K27me3 developmental targets (WNT7A, PAX2 and GATA4) occurred among upregulated transcripts, while distal closing elements were enriched for TFAP2, TEAD, RUNX, FOX and GATA motifs alongside reduced TFAP2C and TEAD2 expression. Direct profiling of H3K27me3, H3K4me3 and PRC1/PRC2 occupancy by CUT&RUN or ChIP-seq will be needed to determine whether these changes reflect redistribution of Polycomb complexes across promoters and distal enhancers.

Performing UTY and KDM5D rescue in Y-depleted HT1080 cells provided a direct test of whether restoring Yq-encoded chromatin regulators can reverse this established transcriptional state. On an independently defined 150-gene baseline LOY signature from batch 1, UTY rescue shifted the rank-based score 51% of the way from empty vector toward sgCTRL (and 35% when combined with KDM5D), driven specifically by re-induction of genes downregulated after Y loss, including 36 of the 39 genes that exhibited joint baseline RNA and promoter-accessibility loss. By contrast, KDM5D rescue alone produced no recovery. Two non-mutually exclusive mechanisms may explain this divergence between UTY and KDM5D. First, HT1080 carries a heterozygous IDH1 R132C mutation that produces the oncometabolite D-2-hydroxyglutarate (D-2HG; [Ma et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.18632/oncotarget.3330)), which directly inhibits 2-oxoglutarate-dependent JmjC demethylases including KDM5D ([Gunn et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-22-0825)), whereas UTY acts largely through demethylase-independent scaffold and phase-separation functions ([Shpargel et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1002964); [Walport et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.555052); [Shi et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03903-7)). Second, KDM5D transcript abundance in the lentiviral rescue exceeded endogenous sgCTRL levels by more than 100-fold (compared with ~9-fold for UTY), so supraphysiological KDM5D dosage or irreversibility of H3K4-demethylated states after prolonged culture could limit transcriptional rescue. Testing UTY and KDM5D rescue under titrated, near-endogenous expression in both IDH1-mutant and IDH1-wild-type backgrounds (or in the presence of mutant-IDH1 inhibition), paired with ATAC-seq after rescue, will clarify whether UTY directly reopens closed promoters and whether KDM5D activity is constrained by D-2HG.

### 3.4.5 Study limitations and future directions

Several experimental limitations should be considered when interpreting the molecular phenotypes described in this chapter. First, although repeated sampling across passages (DU145 and HT1080 ATAC-seq), two collection batches separated by nine months (HT1080 RNA-seq) or matched collection occasions (HT1080 rescue) established the temporal stability of differences within each derived pool, each condition was represented by one established population. Consequently, true biological effects of Y depletion cannot be fully separated from subclonal copy-number changes or adaptations acquired during pool expansion, and sgCTRL RNP delivery controls for nucleofection but not for Cas9-induced DNA double-strand breaks. Second, in A549, substantial within-group variation in RNA integrity (RINe) required covariate adjustment for gene-level inference and limited the resolution of unadjusted pathway rankings.

To overcome both subclonal drift and between-sample technical variation in future transcriptomic studies, a powerful design will be to perform three independent biological replicate RNP nucleofections that generate mosaic (partially Y-depleted) pools, maintain Y-null and Y-retaining cells together within the same culture dish, and profile the multiplexed replicates by single-cell RNA-seq (similar to the strategy used in CRISPR-Taiji; [Feng et al., 2025, preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.25.650684)). Classifying each cell's Y-chromosome status directly from its Y-linked transcript counts allows Y-depleted and Y-intact cells that share an identical microenvironment and culture history to be compared within each replicate, eliminating batch effects and clonal divergence. Combining this mosaic single-cell approach across acute and longitudinal time points with non-cleaving centromeric mis-segregation methods ([Bosco et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2023.03.029); [Feng et al., 2025, preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.25.650684)) and single- or double-gene knockouts of UTY and KDM5D will separate immediate responses to chromosome loss from long-term adaptation. Meanwhile, the well-characterized isogenic HT1080, A549 and DU145 populations established here provide a direct experimental foundation for Chapter 4, where we integrate public dependency screens with comparative CRISPR essentiality screens to identify genetic vulnerabilities associated with loss of the Y chromosome.
