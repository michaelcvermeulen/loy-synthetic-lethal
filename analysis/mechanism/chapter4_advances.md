Relative to the current LOY and cancer functional-genomics literature, Chapter 4 makes five main scientific and methodological advances:

### 1. First Genome-Wide CRISPR Synthetic-Lethality Screens in Matched Isogenic LOY Models
Prior functional studies of LOY in cancer (e.g., Köferle et al., 2022; Shohat et al., 2022; Qi et al., 2023) relied on correlative comparisons across heterogeneous, unmatched cell-line panels (DepMap/Project Score) and targeted validation of known X–Y gametologs, while recent isogenic chromosome-arm-loss screens (Desjardins et al., 2026) examined autosomal arms (13q, 18q) in female lines. 
* **The advance:** [Sections 4.3.1](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L73-L186) and [4.3.5](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L272-L320) present the first genome-wide pooled knockout screens comparing matched Y-depleted and Y-retaining populations within shared parental genomes (HT1080 and A549), removing the inter-tumoural genetic background noise that dominates cross-cell-line panels.

### 2. Quantitative Resolution of Why Public Panels Only Recover the Four Core Gametologs
In the literature, it remained ambiguous whether the absence of non-paralog LOY hits in DepMap or drug screens meant that LOY only creates paralog vulnerabilities, or that stratified public cohorts are simply underpowered.
* **The advance:** [Section 4.3.0](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L3-L71) pairs a large-scale reanalysis (568 male CRISPR models, 6,413 pan-cancer drug comparisons, and $>10^6$ lineage- and driver-stratified genetic/pharmacological tests) with **empirical power simulations** transferring 25 benchmark synthetic lethalities (e.g., *WRN*–MSI, *PRMT5*–*MTAP*) onto actual LOY sample splits.
* It demonstrates quantitatively that once public panels are subdivided by cancer type or driver mutation, sample sizes and inter-line variance prevent detection of even benchmark-strength non-paralog vulnerabilities in most subsets. It also extends replication of *DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, and *RPS4X* (and the non-replication of *ZFX*) into 3D malignant organoid and neurosphere cohorts (Sanger and DepMap NextGen).

### 3. Practical Rules Governing Which X–Y Gametolog Scores in a Given Background
Rather than treating *DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, *RPS4X*, and *ZFX* as interchangeable markers of LOY synthetic lethality, [Sections 4.3.1.2](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L101-L152), [4.3.5](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L290-L291), and [4.4.2](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_discussion.md#L7-L12) show how biological baseline state and screen kinetics determine paralog recovery:
* **Parental baseline dependency & Y dosage:** HT1080 is unusually tolerant of *DDX3X* loss at baseline (`Chronos +0.18`), allowing Y loss to unmask *DDX3X* as the strongest genome-wide sensitizer (`normZ = -11.45`). Conversely, parental A549 already has low Y expression and strong baseline *DDX3X* dependency (`Chronos -0.98`), leaving little dynamic range for *DDX3X* but recovering *EIF1AX* (`rank 8`).
* **Dropout kinetics & partial Y retention:** Core housekeeping genes (*EIF1AX*, *RPS4X*) deplete to the sequencing count floor during pre-D0 antibiotic selection in HT1080 (compounded by retained distal Yp *RPS4Y1* expression), explaining why endpoint-only pooled screens miss pan-essential paralog pairs unless sampled early or screened with inducible/CRISPRi systems.

### 4. Nominating Non-Gametolog Coactivator & Metabolic Vulnerabilities—and Separating LOY-Unique from Convergent Aneuploid Stress
In HT1080, Y depletion nominates coordinated non-paralog vulnerabilities across **six of 20 SAGA subunits** (*TAF6L*, *TADA2B*, *SUPT20H*, *ATXN7L3*, *TADA1*, *TAF5L*) plus Mediator (*MED12*), **SREBP lipid and selenoprotein pathways** (*SREBF2*, *SREBF1*, *SCLY*, *ACSL4*), and X-linked non-gametologs (*TIMM8A*, *BCORL1*), while A549 nominates *L3MBTL2* (PRC1.6) and *UBR5*. Two integrative analyses elevate this beyond a standard candidate list:
* **Multi-omic mechanism exclusion ([Section 4.3.1.3](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L187-L192)):** Integrating Chapter 3 RNA-seq, promoter ATAC-seq, and *UTY*/*KDM5D* rescue profiles proves that these dependencies are **not** caused by baseline transcriptional silencing or promoter closure of the candidate genes themselves ($\rho = -0.005$), pointing instead to increased functional reliance on coactivator complexes amidst downregulated MYC target programs.
* **Cross-screen specificity ([Section 4.3.6](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L321-L330)):** Reprocessing 27 published isogenic CRISPR contrasts (Desjardins et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2022) shows that *DDX3X* sensitization is strictly unique to LOY (0/27 external contrasts), whereas SAGA sensitization recurs in specific non-LOY contexts such as 13q arm loss and *KEAP1* mutation. This provides a nuanced, field-correct framing: SAGA is not a "Y-only" target, but a convergent chromatin-coactivator vulnerability exposed by select aneuploid and oncogenic states.

### 5. Methodological Advances in Isogenic Screen QC and Reciprocal Spectral Competition Assays
* **Cross-screen guide artefact and baseline-adjustment audit ([Section 4.2.3](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_methods.md#L61-L78), [S4.3](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_supplementary_methods.md#L53-L70), [4.3.6](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L329-L330)):** You identify systematic Brunello guide non-detection (`TCTC`/`AGACG` 5′ prefixes) and recurring PCR-jackpotted guides shared between HT1080 D0 and A549 P3 ($\rho = 0.997$), and show across 29 isogenic screens that T0/D0 baseline divergence quantitatively explains when endpoint-only (`drugZ`) and baseline-adjusted (`IsogenicZ`) rankings diverge ($\rho = 0.61$).
* **Reciprocal spectral flow-cytometry competition assay ([Sections 4.3.2–4.3.4](file:///A:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter4/chapter4_results.md#L203-L269)):** Combining reciprocal `eGFP`/`mCherry` dye-swap co-cultures, spectral autofluorescence unmixing (`BD FACSDiscover A8`), within-well D0 log-odds normalization, and same-genotype controls solves a major problem in validating moderate fitness hits in heterogeneous or slow-growing aneuploid lines—confirming early LOY-selective fitness penalties for *DDX3X* and *TADA2B* while revealing a baseline proliferative advantage of unperturbed LOY HT1080 cells in co-culture.
