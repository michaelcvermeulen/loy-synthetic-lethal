Against the current LOY and chromosome-engineering literature, Chapter 3 makes **five main advances**—spanning methodology, structural genomics, comparative transcriptomics, and chromatin mechanism:

### 1. T2T-Y-informed, clone-free pooled LOY engineering across human cancer lineages
* **The field limitation:** Previous experimental LOY models either designed guides against the gapped GRCh38 assembly, relied on integrated plasmid/lentiviral Cas9 (risking continual cutting and chronic DNA-damage signalling across repetitive arrays), or isolated single-cell clones and spontaneous sublines (e.g., Celli et al., 2024; Dorsch et al., 2026; Schlüter et al., 2026; Waterhölter et al., 2026). In genetically unstable cancer cell lines, single-cell cloning fixes pre-existing subclonal copy-number and transcriptional heterogeneity that can rival or obscure the effect of losing chromosome Y.
* **The advance ([Sections 3.3.1–3.3.2](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L7-L55)):** By exhaustively scanning the complete HG002 T2T-Y assembly with strict non-Y mismatch filtering, you identified high-copy repeat guides across centromeric DYZ3 ($\alpha$-satellite; CP, CN), Yq12 DYZ1/HSat3 (Q53), and Yp TSPY arrays (P47, P51). Delivering combined centromeric + Yq12 (CP + Q53) Cas9 RNPs transiently across two successive nucleofections—or in a single round after transient HDAC inhibition with panobinostat ([Section 3.3.2.4](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L347-L349))—achieves 88–99% Yq depletion directly in **bulk pooled populations** without single-cell cloning or transgene integration. You also show empirically that reference repeat count does not predict efficacy (CP vs. CN within the same DYZ3 array).

### 2. Exposing partial Yp retention as a recurring outcome of Y cleavage (and a blind spot of single-locus LOY assays)
* **The field limitation:** Both experimental and clinical studies routinely rely on single-locus PCR/ddPCR (most commonly *AMELY*/*AMELX*) to confirm "complete LOY."
* **The advance ([Sections 3.3.2.1–3.3.2.3](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L120-L257)):** By combining multiplex ddPCR with T2T-Y-aligned, Y-PAR-masked lpWGS and regional RNA-seq, you demonstrate that while A549 loses the entire Y chromosome across both arms, HT1080 and especially DU145 completely eliminate euchromatic Yq (*USP9Y*–*EIF1AY*, <1% retention) and proximal Yp (*AMELY* at ~6.5 Mb, 96–98% loss in DU145) yet selectively retain a distal male-specific Yp segment (~2.46–4.80 Mb) containing *RPS4Y1* and *ZFY* (20–36% expression in HT1080; 46–54% DNA and RNA retention in DU145).
* **Why it matters:** This proves that standard *AMELY*/*AMELX* assays misclassify partial Y deletions as whole-chromosome loss, and it establishes a critical functional distinction between fully lost Yq paralogs (*DDX3Y*, *EIF1AY*, *UTY*, *KDM5D*) and partially retained distal Yp paralogs (*RPS4Y1*, *ZFY*) when interpreting paralog synthetic lethality in Chapter 4.

### 3. Reconciling the "widespread rewiring vs. minimal autosomal effect" debate in the LOY literature
* **The field limitation:** Recent studies have reported seemingly contradictory phenotypes—ranging from extensive autosomal and EMT rewiring in engineered or natural clones (Celli et al., 2024; Schlüter et al., 2026; Dorsch et al., 2026) to virtually no autosomal transcriptional effect beyond sex-chromosome dosage in renal cell carcinoma (Waterhölter et al., 2026).
* **The advance ([Sections 3.3.3.1–3.3.3.4](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L401-L976)):** Profiling three distinct cancer lineages in parallel—and benchmarking them against 18 external Y-loss/mosaic/45,X contrasts—resolves this tension by separating sex-chromosome dosage, gene-level lineage specificity, and pathway-level convergence:
  1. **Universal absence of X-paralog transcriptional compensation:** All three models show 23–33% reductions in summed PAR1 expression and zero compensatory upregulation of X-linked paralogs (*DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, *RPS4X*, *ZFX*, *KDM6A*, *KDM5C*), explaining how Y loss directly creates paralog dosage vulnerabilities.
  2. **Strong lineage dependence at the individual gene level:** Pairwise autosomal fold-change correlations across HT1080, A549, and DU145 are near zero ($\rho = -0.06$ to $0.11$), with ~1,000 DEGs in HT1080, 147 DEGs in DU145 (marked by copy-number-stable suppression of *GRHL2* and luminal epithelial markers alongside basement-membrane collagen induction, without canonical EMT TF activation), and only 10 non-Y/non-PAR DEGs in A549.
  3. **Conserved cell-intrinsic suppression of interferon and inflammatory signalling:** Despite gene-level divergence, pathway enrichment converges on downregulated type I/II interferon and inflammatory responses (plus rRNA processing) across your models, naturally Y-mosaic single-cell datasets, MB49 tumours, 45,X models, and *UTY*-knockout hESCs. Observing this in pure cancer cultures demonstrates that Y loss dampens **cell-intrinsic** basal innate immune signalling independently of infiltrating immune cells.

### 4. Separating Y-specific regulation from general aneuploidy and culture-adaptation effects
* **The field limitation:** Most LOY transcriptomic studies compare a single time point without testing whether observed pathway changes simply reflect a generic stress response to chromosome/arm loss or clonal drift over time.
* **The advance ([Section 3.3.3.5](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L1006-L1008)):** By systematically comparing your models against 16 public chromosome- and arm-loss contrasts (acute RPTEC Yq vs. 3p loss, ReDACT trisomy corrections, and RPE1 monosomies), you show that ribosomal-protein downregulation is a shared hallmark of autosomal monosomy/arm loss, that acute Yq and 3p loss share p53 activation and E2F suppression but diverge in type I interferon signalling, and that interferon and E2F signatures can invert between early (days 5–18) and late (day 90) culture. Additionally, tracking lpWGS alongside RNA-seq and ATAC-seq explicitly separates copy-number-driven regional expression shifts (e.g., 20p in DU145, 13q/4 in HT1080) from copy-number-independent chromatin and transcriptional regulation.

### 5. Linking Y depletion to Polycomb-associated promoter closing and demonstrating *UTY*-dependent reversibility
* **The field limitation:** Although *UTY* and *KDM5D* are widely proposed to mediate epigenetic remodelling in LOY tumours, direct causal rescue of the LOY transcriptome in human cancer cells—integrated with chromatin accessibility—has been lacking.
* **The advance ([Sections 3.3.3.1](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L544-L669) and [3.3.4](file:///a:/Dropbox/LOY_paralogs/projects/SEPT_11_2026_THESIS/draftv2/chapter3/chapter3_results.md#L1030-L1070)):**
  * **Chromatin compaction:** In HT1080, ATAC-seq reveals an ~7:1 skew toward chromatin closing (663 closed vs. 95 opened peaks) that is independent of autosomal copy number ($\rho = -0.05$). Downregulated genes are >30-fold more likely to lose promoter accessibility than non-DE genes, TSS accessibility drops by 21% even across downregulated genes without a significant peak-level call, and jointly closed/repressed promoters are significantly enriched for PRC2/H3K27me3 targets after matching for baseline accessibility, GC content, and CpG density.
  * **Causal rescue by *UTY*:** Re-expressing *UTY* (alone or with *KDM5D*) in Y-depleted HT1080 cells reverses 51% (and 35%) of an independently defined batch-1 150-gene LOY signature, driven specifically by re-inducing genes that were silenced and underwent promoter closing after Y loss (36 of 39 joint RNA/ATAC-loss genes increase after *UTY* rescue), whereas *KDM5D* overexpression alone produces no recovery. This demonstrates that a major component of the LOY transcriptional state is **actively maintained and reversible** through a *UTY*-responsive chromatin mechanism rather than irreversible clonal selection.
