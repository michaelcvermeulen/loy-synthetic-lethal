# ZMYND8 follow-up, 11 September 2026

Requested after the primary analysis, so this is a post hoc mechanistic investigation. The figure and complete tables are regenerated by `zmynd8_followup()` and `zmynd8_figure()` in the ordinary runner.

The combined CollecTRI ULM score is +4.4565 (target-pattern BH 0.000529 across 687 regulators), August +5.3209 and May +2.7133. May BH is 0.0751. All eight resource targets are expressed; coverage is complete but small. ZMYND8 RNA changes by only +0.0526 log2 units overall, with opposite small period changes. KDM5D RNA is depleted (-7.497), while KDM5C is near unchanged (-0.0505).

Six of the eight network edges are repressive. MMP1 and MMP3 are strongly down in both periods and support a positive inferred score. Removing MMP1 reduces the combined score to +1.2865; removing MMP3 gives +2.8286; removing both gives -0.6996. The expression background is retained during edge omission. All eight single-target omissions, six matched signatures and leave-one-match-out scores are exported. Omission P values are unadjusted exploratory diagnostics, not a second set of confirmatory tests. Absolute signed-effect shares are descriptive magnitude shares, not a decomposition of the ULM t statistic.

The focused co-occupied genes in the KDM5D/JARID1D study are SNAI2/Slug, CD44, VEGFA and EGFR. Here CD44 and EGFR increase modestly; SNAI2 and VEGFA are near unchanged. The score therefore should be described as a small, MMP-driven ZMYND8-like target pattern, without asserting globally increased ZMYND8 activity or functional compensation for KDM5D loss. Other regulators share these target genes and can generate the same pattern. The frozen CollecTRI file contains source, target and sign but no edge-specific study references; provenance cannot be reconstructed beyond the frozen network from this file alone.

All six matched signatures have positive ZMYND8 scores (1.59-4.06), and each leave-one-match-out combined score stays positive (4.15-4.51). Both MMP genes decrease in all six matches. This supports within-population temporal consistency while leaving target specificity unresolved. FOS, JUN and ETS1 have activating edges to both MMP1 and MMP3 in the same network; their scores and shared edges are exported. This shared evidence can reflect reduced activating input as well as increased repressive input and does not identify a unique regulator.

The user-supplied PII S0969212617302903 is Yao et al., *Structure* 2017, on the ZMYND8-Drebrin complex and possible cytoplasmic sequestration, not the direct KDM5D repression experiment. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28966017/)

Direct KDM5D/JARID1D-ZMYND8 interaction, histone-mark recognition and repression were demonstrated by Li et al., *Molecular Cell* 2016, in prostate cancer models. This supports the biological connection, but transfer to HT1080 requires testing. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4975651/)

ZMYND8/RACK7-KDM5C enhancer regulation was demonstrated by Shen et al., *Cell* 2016, in a different experimental context. This makes KDM5C a plausible partner to investigate, not an established compensation mechanism here. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4826479/)

ZMYND8 can also support HIF-associated oncogenic transcription in breast cancer, reinforcing that its effects depend on partner and cell context. [Chen et al., JCI 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29629903/)

Proposed discriminating experiments: ZMYND8/KDM5C protein and nuclear localization; ZMYND8 depletion in both established populations with an eight-target expression panel; KDM5D addback under a confirmed intervention design; then occupancy/histone-mark profiling and functional invasion measurements if the transcriptional effect depends on ZMYND8. No addback, protein, chromatin or invasion experiment was performed in this RNA follow-up. Independent population generation remains necessary for generalizing LOY effects.
