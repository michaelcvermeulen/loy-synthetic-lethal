# Methods

## Cell culture

HT1080 fibrosarcoma cells (ATCC, CCL-121) were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiotic–antimycotic solution at 37 °C in 5% CO2. Cell line identity was authenticated by short tandem repeat profiling, and cultures were confirmed to be free of mycoplasma before the start of the screen.

## Generation and validation of isogenic Y-negative HT1080 cells

Isogenic Y chromosome-negative (LOY) HT1080 cells were generated by multiplexed CRISPR–Cas9 targeting of Y-specific repetitive sequence families, an approach that produces many simultaneous double-strand breaks on the Y chromosome and drives its elimination without requiring a selectable marker. Candidate target sites were identified in the T2T-CHM13v2.0 reference assembly, which provides a gapless representation of the human Y chromosome and therefore permits accurate copy counting within repeat arrays. Candidates were retained only if they occurred at more than ten copies on chrY and had no exact match elsewhere in the genome (chr1–22, chrX and chrM); the remaining sites were ranked by off-target potential, assessed as Cutting Frequency Determination scores across sites bearing one to four mismatches, and by predicted on-target activity using DeepSpCas9 and Rule Set 2. Two guides directed against distinct repeat families were selected for HT1080: CP (5'-ACTATCTTCGCATGAGAACC-3'; AGG PAM; 54 Y-chromosome sites; centromeric repeat) and Q53 (5'-TCGCCAGGAATTGATGTGAA-3'; CGG PAM; 532 Y-chromosome sites; DYZ1/HSat3 array on Yq).

Chemically modified synthetic sgRNAs (Synthego) were complexed with purified SpCas9 2NLS nuclease (Synthego) at equimolar ratios, and ribonucleoprotein complexes were delivered by nucleofection on a Lonza 4D-Nucleofector X Unit using the SF Cell Line Kit and program FF-113. Nucleofected cells were expanded by serial passage and subjected to a second round of nucleofection to enrich for complete Y loss. Isogenic control (CTRL) populations were generated in parallel by nucleofection with a non-targeting sgRNA (Synthego Negative Control SpCas9 gRNA #2), so that both arms of the screen experienced identical nucleofection and expansion histories and differed only in Y chromosome status.

Loss of the Y chromosome was verified by two orthogonal assays. Y chromosome copy number was quantified by droplet digital PCR on a Bio-Rad QX200 system using two independent Y-linked targets, AMELY (assayed against AMELX using the TaqMan Copy Number Assay C_990000001_10) and DDX3Y (normalised to RNaseP), with ratios corrected for the single X chromosome complement of HT1080; LOY populations gave Y ratios approaching zero, against approximately 1.0 in CTRL populations. Loss was independently confirmed at the transcript level by RNA sequencing of LOY and Y-positive HT1080 cells, performed by Azenta Life Sciences on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (polyA-selected libraries, 2 × 150 bp paired-end, approximately 30 million read pairs per sample) with alignment to GRCh38 using STAR. LOY populations showed complete loss of expression of the Y-linked genes *RPS4Y1*, *DDX3Y*, *KDM5D*, *ZFY*, *USP9Y*, *EIF1AY*, *UTY* and *NLGN4Y* relative to Y-positive controls. Low-pass whole-genome sequencing to confirm loss at the DNA level and to survey for incidental chromosomal alterations is in progress.

## CRISPR knockout library and transduction

Screening was performed with the Human Brunello genome-wide CRISPR knockout library (Broad GPP; Addgene #73178-LV), comprising 76,441 sgRNAs targeting 19,114 protein-coding genes at four guides per gene together with 1,000 non-targeting control (NTC) sgRNAs, for a total of 77,441 constructs. Lentivirus was obtained from Addgene at a titre of at least 1 × 10^7 transducing units (TU)/mL.

Multiplicity of infection (MOI) was optimised separately in LOY and CTRL cells by transducing six-well plates with a range of virus volumes (5, 10, 15, 20 and 30 µL), selecting in 5 µg/mL puromycin for three days, and scoring survival by microscopy. Transduction efficiency was lower than expected across the titration, so a target MOI of 0.4 was adopted rather than the more usual 0.3; this remains low enough that the large majority of transduced cells carry a single integrated sgRNA while improving the yield of transduced cells.

For the screen proper, 300 million cells of each genotype were transduced at MOI 0.4 with 8 mL of Brunello lentivirus in medium supplemented with Pluronic F-108 (5 µL/mL) and polybrene (10 µL/mL). Twenty-four hours after transduction, cells were placed under puromycin selection at 5 µg/mL; selection was increased to 10 µg/mL on day 5 to eliminate residual untransduced cells, and continued for a total of eight days. Library representation was maintained at 400× throughout, corresponding to approximately 31 million cells per replicate (77,441 sgRNAs × 400).

## Screen design and passaging

At the completion of puromycin selection (day 8), a baseline (D0) sample was harvested from each genotype to record library composition before competitive outgrowth. The remaining cells were divided into two biological replicates per genotype, giving four arms (CTRL-Rep1, CTRL-Rep2, LOY-Rep1, LOY-Rep2). Each replicate was seeded across ten plates at 3.1 million cells per plate, for 31 million cells and 400× coverage per replicate and 40 plates in total. Replicates were passaged every three to four days with 32 million cells re-plated at each passage, so that coverage never fell below 400×. Cells were carried through four passages, corresponding to a cumulative 9.3–9.8 population doublings per replicate (LOY-Rep1, 9.27; LOY-Rep2, 9.58; CTRL-Rep1, 9.78; CTRL-Rep2, 9.47). At the final passage, cells were trypsinised and counted, and approximately half of each harvest (60–90 million cells, depending on replicate) was pelleted and stored at −20 °C for genomic DNA extraction.

## Genomic DNA extraction and sequencing library preparation

Genomic DNA was extracted from frozen cell pellets using the Blood and Cell Culture DNA Maxi Kit (Qiagen, #13362) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Integrated sgRNA cassettes were recovered by a two-step PCR adapted from Joung and colleagues (doi:10.1016/j.xpro.2022.101579). To preserve library representation, PCR1 was performed at one reaction per 2,500 sgRNAs, giving 31 reactions of 10 µg genomic DNA each, or 310 µg of input DNA per sample. Each 100 µL PCR1 reaction contained 10 µg genomic DNA, 1 µM each of the cassette-flanking primers (forward 5'-TCATATGCTTACCGTAACTTGAAA-3'; reverse 5'-AACTTCTCGGGGACTGTGG-3') and 1× ExtremeTaq 2× Master Mix, and was cycled at 98 °C for 2 min, followed by 18 cycles of 98 °C for 30 s, 59.1 °C for 30 s and 72 °C for 45 s, with a final extension of 72 °C for 3 min.

PCR1 products were pooled and 10 µL carried into a 100 µL PCR2 that appended Illumina P5 and P7 flow-cell sequences together with sample-specific 8 bp dual indexes (TruSeq CD index set), using 0.5 µM each of the i5 and i7 primers and 1× ExtremeTaq 2× Master Mix under the same cycling profile for 20 cycles. Indexes were assigned as follows: CTRL-Rep1 (D701/ATTACTCG, D501/TATAGCCT), CTRL-Rep2 (D703/CGCTCATT, D503/CCTATCCT), LOY-Rep1 (D704/GAGATTCC, D504/GGCTCTGA), LOY-Rep2 (D705/ATTCAGAA, D505/AGGCGAAG), D0-CTRL (D702/TCCGGAGA, D502/ATAGAGGC) and D0-LOY (D706/GAATTCGT, D506/TAATCTTA).

PCR2 products were resolved on a 2% agarose gel, the approximately 400 bp product excised, and DNA recovered with the Monarch DNA Gel Extraction Kit (NEB, #T1020S); gel purification was performed twice to remove residual primer-dimer and non-specific product. Final libraries were quantified by Qubit and sized by TapeStation (D1000 ScreenTape), giving a mean fragment size of 474 bp (range 336–1,010 bp) with 96.1% of signal in the target region, and concentrations of 56.8 ng/µL (155 nM) by Qubit and 49.8 ng/µL (146 nM) by TapeStation. The observed size is consistent with the 470–480 bp product expected after two-step amplification and adapter addition.

## Sequencing

The six indexed libraries were sequenced by Azenta Life Sciences on an Illumina platform in 2 × 150 bp paired-end mode with a 25% PhiX spike-in, targeting 120 million reads per library, and returned as demultiplexed FASTQ files. Sequencing yielded 1.85 billion reads in total (555,193 Mbases) at a mean quality score of 39.6, with 98.07% of bases at or above Q30. Per-sample read counts were 263.7 million (D0-CTRL), 169.8 million (D0-LOY), 268.5 million (CTRL-Rep1), 332.5 million (CTRL-Rep2), 525.4 million (LOY-Rep1) and 290.8 million (LOY-Rep2), corresponding to nominal depths of approximately 2,200–6,900 reads per sgRNA.

## Read processing and sgRNA quantification

Raw reads were assessed with FastQC (v0.11.9) and summarised across samples with MultiQC (v1.14), with per-base quality, duplication level and adapter content inspected for each library. Because the sgRNA spacer lies entirely within read 1 of the amplicon, only R1 reads were used for counting and R2 reads were discarded. The position of the spacer relative to the constant vector sequence was determined empirically by scanning a range of 5' trim lengths and selecting the value that maximised the fraction of reads matching the library; a fixed 5' trim of 22 nt was used for all samples.

sgRNA abundances were quantified with the MAGeCK (v0.5.9.4) `count` module against the Brunello library definition (77,441 sgRNAs) with `--trim-5 22`. Counts were normalised to the 1,000 NTC sgRNAs (`--norm-method control --control-sgrna`) rather than to total reads, so that normalisation is anchored to constructs not expected to respond to Y chromosome status. The same control-based normalisation was applied at every subsequent stage of the analysis.

## Count-matrix quality control

Count-matrix quality was assessed in R using custom scripts that evaluated library size, the fraction of the library detected, the Gini coefficient of the count distribution, pairwise Spearman correlation of log-transformed counts between samples, replicate concordance, and clustering by principal component analysis. All four endpoint libraries performed well, with 84.8–85.6% of reads assigned to library sgRNAs, Gini coefficients of 0.080–0.087, and fewer than 713 undetected sgRNAs out of 77,441 (more than 99.0% of the library detected).

The two D0 libraries behaved differently. Although their evenness was comparable to the endpoint libraries (Gini 0.114; 363 and 365 undetected sgRNAs), only 21.2% (D0-CTRL) and 21.5% (D0-LOY) of their reads mapped to the library. The unmapped fraction was investigated by extracting non-matching reads and querying them against GRCh38 and against alternative sgRNA library definitions; the pattern is consistent with a library-preparation artefact affecting the two D0 amplicons specifically rather than with loss of library complexity, since representation itself was preserved. Because their low mapping rate makes the D0 libraries an unreliable denominator, the primary analysis excludes D0 and compares the endpoint LOY and CTRL populations directly. This is defensible because the two arms are isogenic, were transduced with the same virus preparation in parallel, and were carried through an identical passage schedule, so the CTRL endpoint serves as a matched reference for library composition after outgrowth. As a sensitivity analysis, the D0 libraries were retained in a parallel set of models after removing the 5% of sgRNAs with the highest D0 counts (3,872 of 77,441 guides removed; 73,569 retained), which improved MAGeCK MLE normalisation factors to a range of 0.79–1.13 while preserving the essential-gene depletion signal.

As an internal positive control, depletion of reference core-essential genes was tested in each endpoint arm using the Achilles common-essential and non-essential control sets, comparing the log fold-change distribution of essential genes against that of non-essential genes.

## Identification of LOY-specific dependencies

The primary comparison was the endpoint LOY population against the endpoint CTRL population (POST_LOY_REP1 and POST_LOY_REP2 versus POST_CTRL_REP1 and POST_CTRL_REP2), in which depletion of a gene's sgRNAs in LOY relative to CTRL identifies a candidate Y-loss-specific dependency and enrichment identifies a candidate suppressor.

Hit calling was performed primarily with drugZ, which was developed for two-condition genetic-interaction and chemogenetic screens and estimates guide-level variance empirically from a sliding window across the abundance range, making it better suited than rank-based methods to designs with few replicates. drugZ internally rescales each library to 10 million reads, computes a guide-level log2 fold-change between conditions with a pseudocount, converts each fold-change to a z-score against the local empirical variance, and aggregates guides to a gene-level normZ statistic as the sum of guide z-scores divided by the square root of the number of observations. One-sided p-values for synthetic lethality (fdr_synth) and for suppression (fdr_supp) were corrected for multiple testing by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure across 19,084 genes. Genes with FDR < 0.25 were retained as candidates; 48 genes met this threshold for synthetic lethality.

Because two biological replicates per arm afford limited power, a deliberately permissive threshold of FDR < 0.25 combined with an absolute log fold-change greater than 0.5 was used throughout for candidate nomination. This screen is intended as a hypothesis-generating experiment, and candidates were prioritised for orthogonal validation rather than treated as individually confirmed dependencies.

Four additional methods were run on the same count matrix as orthogonal support, and agreement across methods was the main criterion for prioritisation. MAGeCK `test` was used to compute robust rank aggregation (RRA) statistics for the primary comparison and, as secondary contrasts, for the CTRL and LOY time courses and the D0 baseline comparison, with control-sgRNA normalisation and gene-level fold-changes summarised by the alpha-median method. MAGeCK `mle` was used to fit generalised linear models across all six libraries under three designs: a simple LOY-effect model, a model including time, genotype and their interaction, and a model including replicate as a batch covariate; gene-level beta scores and Wald FDRs were extracted from each. BAGEL2 was used to compute Bayes factors for essentiality in each genotype separately against reference essential and non-essential training sets, and the difference in Bayes factor between genotypes (ΔBF = BF_LOY − BF_CTRL) was used as a genotype-specific essentiality score. Finally, CRISPRcleanR was applied to correct for gene-independent responses to Cas9 cutting arising from copy-number amplification, by identifying genomic segments of uniform sgRNA log fold-change and normalising within them; the corrected counts were then re-analysed with MAGeCK so that the effect of copy-number correction on the candidate list could be assessed directly.

## Pathway and complex enrichment

Genes were ranked by a signed significance statistic, −log10(FDR) × sign(log fold-change), and tested for coordinate depletion or enrichment by pre-ranked gene set enrichment analysis using fgsea, with gene sets retrieved through msigdbr for the MSigDB Hallmark collection, the KEGG canonical pathway collection and the Gene Ontology Cellular Component collection, the last used to test for coordinate behaviour of physical protein complexes. Analyses used a minimum set size of 15, a maximum of 500, 10,000 permutations and a fixed random seed, with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment across gene sets and a significance threshold of adjusted p < 0.25 to match the gene-level threshold. Candidate depleted and enriched gene lists were additionally submitted to Metascape for ontology enrichment and MCODE-based detection of densely connected protein–protein interaction modules.
