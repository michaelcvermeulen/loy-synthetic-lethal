Experimental design and expression structure of the HT1080 add-back experiment. **a,** The Y-retaining sgCTRL and untransduced Y-depleted populations provided contextual references. Empty-vector, UTY, KDM5D and combined UTY–KDM5D populations were produced by separate transductions from the same starting Y-depleted pool. Each established population was sampled on three matched collection occasions; these observations are not independent transductions. **b,** Reserved panel for the author-confirmed UTY and KDM5D Western blots. Original images, lane identities, loading controls and exposure information must be checked before final insertion; the current export contains a labelled placeholder. **c,** UTY and KDM5D RNA abundance in all six populations on a shared log2 CPM axis, calculated from raw counts using the primary TMM effective library sizes and a prior count of two. Points show individual samples, shapes identify collection occasion and horizontal bars show group means. Open symbols in the shaded band are samples with no reads for that gene. Dashed lines mark the sgCTRL mean for each gene. Numbers give the fold difference in geometric-mean abundance from sgCTRL for populations carrying the corresponding construct. The key below the axis shows which constructs each population carries. Counts include transgene-derived reads. Low KDM5D RNA signal in the UTY-only population remained far below the KDM5D-containing populations. **d,** Unadjusted PCA of TMM-normalised log2 counts per million for 23,992 expressed autosomal and non-pseudoautosomal X-linked genes, centred without variance scaling. PC1 and PC2 explain 37.3% and 14.5% of variance. Colours identify the six established populations and shapes identify collection occasion. The final construct labels follow the laboratory-confirmed four-way relabelling record.

## main - Movement of a frozen HT1080 LOY expression signature after add-back

**Figure:** `ch03_main_ht1080_addback_baseline_reversal` | **Status:** current thesis analysis

[PDF](figures/chapter3/ch03_main_ht1080_addback_baseline_reversal.pdf) | [SVG](figures/chapter3/ch03_main_ht1080_addback_baseline_reversal.svg) | [PNG](figures/chapter3/ch03_main_ht1080_addback_baseline_reversal.png)

Expression of an August-defined LOY signature across the May add-back populations. **a,** Within-sample signature scores for 100 genes frozen using the August comparison of the established Y-depleted and sgCTRL populations: 50 higher- and 50 lower-expression genes. Scores are the mean percentile rank of August-up genes minus the mean percentile rank of August-down genes across the frozen discovery background. Points are individual samples, shapes identify the three matched collection occasions and horizontal bars show group means. **b,** Differences in signature score for the five construct comparisons central to interpretation. Points and intervals are blocked-model estimates and 95% confidence intervals; displayed P values are two-sided moderated-t P values. Benjamini–Hochberg values across all 15 score contrasts are retained in the source table. **c,** Group-mean log2 expression differences from sgCTRL for the 75 frozen-signature genes passing the May primary expression filter. Rows follow the frozen August direction and effect ordering; values are clipped to [−4, 4] for display. The heatmap shows continuous trajectories and does not classify genes as rescued. **d,** Directional `fry` gene-set tests for the 38 August-up and 37 August-down genes passing the May primary expression filter. Columns explicitly state the expected reversal directions: decrease for August-up genes and increase for August-down genes. Rows are comparisons of the first named population relative to the second; LXHA denotes empty vector. Every cell gives the observed direction and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR adjusted across all 12 displayed tests. Blue indicates decrease and orange increase; color does not encode significance. Bold text marks FDR < 0.05. Coordinated shifts do not establish restoration of every gene or causal rescue. The May samples did not select the signature, but August and May repeatedly sampled the same original baseline populations. Each add-back group represents one established population, so the results do not estimate variability among independently generated transductions.

Sensitivity analyses and Y-linked expression context. **a,** Numbers of genes selected using the collection-occasion-blocked nonzero-effect test followed by an absolute log2 fold-change filter of 0.5, blocked TREAT at 0.5 and an unblocked model with the nonzero-effect filter. ## supp - Correlation sensitivity and construct specificity of add-back pathway tests

Competitive CAMERA gene-set tests for the HT1080 add-back populations. **a,** Numbers of MSigDB Hallmark (50 tested), Reactome (1,340) and GO Biological Process (4,684) sets with FDR <0.05 for six contrasts under three inter-gene correlation settings: fixed 0.01 (primary), fixed 0.03 (the median of per-set estimates) and estimated separately for each set. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied within each collection, contrast and setting. **b,** Signed CAMERA z statistics for the 50 Hallmark sets, comparing UTY versus empty vector with untransduced Y-depleted versus the same empty vector (fixed correlation 0.01). The dashed line marks identity. Colour shows which comparisons reached FDR <0.05; the eight sets with the largest UTY statistics are labelled. **c,** Spearman correlations of signed CAMERA statistics between each add-back-versus-vector contrast and naive Y-depleted versus vector, by collection (fixed correlation 0.01). Labels give the number of sets significant for the add-back contrast that were also significant, in the same direction, for naive Y-depleted versus vector. None were significant in the opposite direction for UTY or combined re-expression. Each group is one established population sampled on three matched collection occasions; pathway results are conditional on these populations. Complete per-set results are in the source tables.
