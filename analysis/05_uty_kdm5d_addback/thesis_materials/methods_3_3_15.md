### 3.3.15 HT1080 UTY and KDM5D re-expression RNA analysis

The add-back experiment comprised Y-retaining sgCTRL, untransduced Y-depleted, empty-vector, UTY, KDM5D and combined UTY–KDM5D populations, sampled on three matched collection occasions. The four plasmid-bearing populations were generated by separate transductions of the same starting Y-depleted pool. Each condition was represented by one established population; the three RNA samples per population were repeated collection occasions rather than independent transductions. Final construct labels followed the laboratory-confirmed four-way relabelling record. UTY and KDM5D abundance was assessed by Western blotting and by RNA-seq counts. Protein abundance was used to verify construct expression, not catalytic activity.

The thesis analysis used the original integer featureCounts tables and the GENCODE v49 annotation used for counting. Genes without symbols, chromosome-Y genes and features overlapping the GRCh38 pseudoautosomal regions were excluded from the primary analysis. Counts were filtered using `filterByExpr` with minimum count 10 and minimum total count 15 across the six groups, retaining 23,992 autosomal and non-PAR X-linked genes. TMM normalisation was fitted across all 18 libraries. Y-linked and pseudoautosomal abundance was examined separately using the primary effective library sizes.

Primary differential-expression analysis used limma-voom with a no-intercept six-group design and a fixed effect for matched collection occasion. Robust empirical Bayes moderation was applied. All 15 pairwise group contrasts were retained, and Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the tested genes separately for each contrast. Descriptive selected-gene summaries required FDR <0.05 and absolute estimated log2 fold change at least 0.5; this combined a test against zero with an effect-size filter. TREAT at log2 fold change 0.5 formally tested the magnitude threshold. An unblocked group model, plasmid-only factorial terms and leave-one-collection-occasion-out fits were retained as sensitivity analyses. PCA used TMM-normalised log2 counts per million with prior count 2 across all primary genes, centred by gene without variance scaling.

Baseline reversal used the signature frozen independently of the May add-back measurements in the August HT1080 comparison. Up to 50 genes in each direction were selected from the August-only expression background using an absolute mean matched difference of at least 0.5 and concordant directions across all three August passage comparisons. The frozen set comprised 50 higher- and 50 lower-expression genes. For each May library, raw counts were converted to within-sample percentile ranks across the frozen August discovery background; the signature score was the mean rank of August-up genes minus the mean rank of August-down genes. Scores were analysed with the same group and collection-occasion design. This temporal separation prevented May-dependent gene selection, although both periods repeatedly sampled the same original baseline populations.

Directional `fry` rotation tests assessed the August-up and August-down components for UTY, KDM5D and combined re-expression versus vector, combined versus each single add-back, and naive Y-depleted versus vector. Benjamini–Hochberg correction spanned the resulting 12 tests. Gene-level movement towards sgCTRL was described among frozen-signature genes that passed the May primary expression filter by comparing each group-mean logCPM difference from sgCTRL with the corresponding empty-vector difference. Crossing the sgCTRL mean was recorded separately. These descriptive trajectories were not treated as binary rescue tests.

Competitive gene-set testing used `limma::camera` on the primary voom object, design and observation weights for six contrasts: UTY, KDM5D and combined re-expression versus vector, combined versus each single add-back, and naive Y-depleted versus vector. The gene universe comprised one expressed row per mapped Entrez gene (17,724 genes); for duplicated identifiers, the row with the highest mean logCPM was retained. MSigDB 2026.1.Hs Hallmark, Reactome and GO Biological Process sets were obtained through `msigdbr` and restricted to 10–500 mapped genes, giving 50, 1,340 and 4,684 tested sets. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied within each collection and contrast. The primary setting used a fixed inter-gene correlation of 0.01. As a sensitivity analysis, tests were repeated with correlations estimated separately for each set and with a fixed value of 0.03, the median of those estimates. Construct specificity was assessed by comparing each add-back-versus-vector result with naive Y-depleted versus the same vector. Spearman correlation of signed CAMERA z statistics summarised concordance, and we counted significant sets shared in the same direction.

All results were interpreted as differences among the sampled established populations, conditional on the expression model. Repeated collection occasions did not estimate variability among independently generated add-back populations. The analysis did not use the previous random-gene-set permutation P values, significance-defined “transduction-lost” classes or uniform-effect power simulation. Input hashes, complete contrast tables, sensitivity analyses and figure source tables are retained with the frozen thesis analysis.
