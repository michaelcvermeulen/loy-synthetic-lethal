## 4.2 Materials and Methods

Analysis code will be made available at https://github.com/michaelcvermeulen/loy-synthetic-lethal, and extended technical details and sensitivity analyses are provided in **Supplementary Methods S4.1–S4.5** ([chapter4_supplementary_methods.md](chapter4_supplementary_methods.md)).

### 4.2.1 Public-data analyses of LOY-associated genetic dependencies and drug sensitivities

#### 4.2.1.1 Public datasets and Y-chromosome status classification

To evaluate functional vulnerabilities associated with loss of chromosome Y (LOY) across established cancer cell-line panels before testing our engineered isogenic models, we analyzed the DepMap 26Q1 release, integrating RNA-seq expression, whole-genome and exome copy number, somatic driver annotations, CRISPR (Chronos) and RNAi dependency profiles, and compound-viability screens across male cancer models (excluding non-cancer and engineered lines).

Because annotated donor sex in DepMap does not indicate whether a male-derived cell line retains chromosome Y, we classified Y-chromosome status across 1,143 male cancer models using expression and DNA copy-number measurements of seven broadly expressed male-specific Y (MSY) genes (*DDX3Y*, *EIF1AY*, *KDM5D*, *RPS4Y1*, *USP9Y*, *UTY*, and *ZFY*), with 711 female cell lines serving as an empirical Y-null reference. Expression data used was $\log_2(\mathrm{TPM} + 1)$ values. Each Y gene was considered expressed above background when its expression exceeded the female reference ceiling. Male models were classified as RNA-depleted (LOY) when mean expression across the seven genes was $\le 1.0$ with at most one gene above background, and as RNA-expressed (Y-positive) when mean expression was $\ge 2.5$ with at least six genes above background. When RNA data were unavailable, DNA copy-number ratios for the same genes supplied the call (requiring at least five measured genes, with all ratios $\le 0.03$ for LOY and $\ge 0.20$ for Y-positive). Models with intermediate RNA expression, partial Y deletion, or discordant RNA and DNA evidence were excluded from binary comparisons. Sensitivity analyses using 95th-percentile female background thresholds, $\pm 20\%$ threshold shifts, RNA-only, DNA-only, or strictly concordant calls yielded virtually identical classifications (**Supplementary Methods S4.1.1**).

#### 4.2.1.2 Pan-cancer genetic dependencies, machine-learning prediction, and external replication

To identify pan-cancer genetic dependencies associated with LOY while controlling for tissue of origin and CRISPR library differences, we analyzed 17,205 genes measured in at least 90% of the 568 Y-classified male CRISPR models (230 LOY and 338 Y-positive) using `limma` with robust empirical-Bayes moderation and a mean–variance trend:

$$
\mathrm{Chronos}_g \sim \mathrm{OncotreeLineage} + \mathrm{LibraryKY} + \mathrm{LibraryHumagneCD} + Y,
$$

where $Y = 1$ denotes LOY and $Y = 0$ denotes Y-positive models, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across all 17,205 genes. Sensitivity models additionally adjusted for whole-genome doubling, *TP53* status, and aneuploidy.

To extend the Boruta random-forest discovery framework from Chapter 2 to the updated DepMap 26Q1 cohort as a complementary non-parametric approach, we applied Boruta feature selection (`ranger`, 500 trees, 100 iterations) across 6,037 selectively essential genes, permuting shadow features within cancer lineage to identify dependencies whose predictive importance for Y status significantly exceeded chance ($P_{\mathrm{Bonferroni}} < 0.01$; **Supplementary Methods S4.1.1**). Conversely, to quantify how much variation in the four established X-linked paralog dependencies (*DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, *RPS4X*, and *ZFX*) is explained by Y status and Y-gene expression beyond lineage and global transcription, we evaluated cumulative random-forest regression models using ten-fold donor-grouped cross-validation repeated three times. Independent replication of the four paralog dependencies was tested in 95 male Sanger malignant-organoid models and 65 male DepMap NextGen organoid and neurosphere models using lineage- and library-adjusted linear and permutation models (**Supplementary Methods S4.1.2**).

#### 4.2.1.3 Context-stratified dependency searches and empirical power simulations

Because vulnerabilities mediated by Y-linked chromatin or transcriptional regulators may be restricted to specific tumour lineages or co-occurring driver backgrounds, we next tested for LOY-associated dependencies across 6,089 selective non-MSY genes within defined biological contexts using rank-normalized Chronos scores in `limma`. Searches were conducted at four levels: (1) within individual Oncotree lineages, primary diseases, and subtypes containing at least five LOY and five Y-positive models; (2) within 47 pan-cancer driver-mutated cohorts ($\ge 5$ models in each $\text{Y status} \times \text{mutation}$ group), pairing within-driver tests with $Y \times \text{driver}$ interaction tests across all models; (3) across 86 binary and continuous genomic contexts (including copy-number amplifications/deletions, molecular subtypes, chromosomal instability, ploidy, and aneuploidy; 523,654 interaction tests); and (4) within 68 combined cancer-type-by-driver strata across 22 cancer types and 14 drivers (414,052 within-altered and 146,136 interaction tests). Context-specific discovery required Benjamini–Hochberg $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$ for both the within-context association and the corresponding interaction term (**Supplementary Methods S4.1.3**).

To determine whether the lack of novel context-specific hits reflected a true absence of vulnerabilities or limited statistical power after subdividing the cell-line panel, we performed power simulations across the 30 eligible cancer-type subsets (at least five models per Y group). First, we introduced synthetic uniform Chronos shifts (0.25 to 10 SD units) into the LOY models of 500 real selective gene profiles per group while preserving observed inter-line variance. Second, we transferred the empirical effect sizes and line-to-line variability of 25 benchmark cancer dependencies (such as *WRN*–MSI and *PRMT5*–*MTAP*) onto the LOY versus Y-positive sample split of each cancer group across 400 resampled replicates, recording the fraction of simulations detected at within-group genome-wide $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$ (**Supplementary Methods S4.1.3**).

#### 4.2.1.4 Pan-cancer and context-stratified drug-sensitivity analyses

To test whether Y loss confers selective pharmacological sensitivity across public compound screens, we compared Y-negative and Y-positive male models across seven datasets (CTRPv2, GDSC1, GDSC2, and four PRISM Repurposing screens: Primary [REP.PRIMARY], 1M [REP.1M], 300 [REP.300], and Secondary; 6,413 compound–dataset comparisons with at least 10 models per Y group) using lineage- and batch-adjusted regression with donor-clustered HC3 robust standard errors. For the 352 compounds assayed in more than one dataset family, responses were pooled using linear mixed-effects models with a random cell-line intercept to account for repeated measurements of the same cell lines across screens. To evaluate class-level shifts across 218 mechanism-of-action and 385 target-gene groups, significance was tested by randomly shuffling the LOY/Y-positive labels within each cancer lineage 2,000 times to build an empirical null distribution (**Supplementary Methods S4.1.4**). *BRAF*-mutant sensitivity to BRAF inhibitors and *TP53*-wild-type sensitivity to MDM2 inhibitors served as positive controls. Within-context drug searches (495,079 tests across 121 lineage and driver subsets with $\ge 5$ models per Y group) and analytical power calculations are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S4.1.4**.

### 4.2.2 Cell culture and genome-wide CRISPR–Cas9 screening in HT1080 and A549

#### 4.2.2.1 Cell lines and matched Y-depleted and control populations

Genome-wide pooled CRISPR–Cas9 knockout screens were performed in the matched Y-depleted (LOY) and non-targeting control (CTRL/sgCTRL) populations of HT1080 fibrosarcoma (ATCC CCL-121) and A549 lung adenocarcinoma (ATCC CCL-185) established and molecularly characterized in Chapter 3 (Sections 3.2.2–3.2.3). Cells were maintained in DMEM (Wisent) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Corning) and 1% antibiotic–antimycotic (Wisent) at 37 °C in 5% $\mathrm{CO}_2$, passaged using trypsin (Wisent), and verified for cell-line identity and mycoplasma negativity as described in Chapter 3. In both backgrounds, the screened populations were uncloned, heterogeneous pools generated by repeated Cas9 RNP nucleofection.

#### 4.2.2.2 Brunello library transduction, shared baseline design, and outgrowth

Both screens used the human Brunello genome-wide CRISPR knockout library in the all-in-one lentiCRISPR v2 vector encoding SpCas9 and 77,441 sgRNAs (76,441 gene-targeting guides across 19,112 genes at $\sim 4$ guides per gene, plus 1,000 non-targeting controls [NTCs]; Addgene #73179-LV; [Sanson et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07901-8)). Following pilot puromycin survival titrations, $3.0 \times 10^8$ cells per genotype ($6.0 \times 10^8$ cells total per screen) were transduced at a target multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.4 in the presence of polybrene and Pluronic F-108 (**Supplementary Methods S4.2.1**). Beginning 24 h after transduction, infected cells were selected in puromycin for eight days (5 µg/mL for four days, then 10 µg/mL for four days).

Following puromycin selection (day 0, D0), each transduced population was sampled for baseline sequencing and split into two parallel outgrowth replicates (`R1` and `R2`), allowing both replicates within a genotype to start from the same initial library representation without doubling the $6.0 \times 10^8$-cell transduction scale. Each outgrowth replicate was maintained at $\ge 400\times$ library coverage by plating $3.2 \times 10^7$ viable cells across ten 15-cm dishes per replicate (40 dishes total across the four arms) at each of four passages (P1–P4). Viable cell yields were measured at each passage using a Countess 2 automated cell counter, and passage population doublings were calculated as:

$$
\mathrm{PD}_j = \log_2\left(\frac{N_{\mathrm{harvested},j}}{N_{\mathrm{plated},j}}\right),
$$

and summed across passages. In HT1080, Yp (*AMELY*/*AMELX*) and Yq (*DDX3Y* or *KDM5D* relative to *RNaseP*) copy numbers were monitored across passages by ddPCR as described in Chapter 3 (Section 3.2.2.3).

#### 4.2.2.3 Genomic DNA extraction and sgRNA library sequencing

Genomic DNA extraction and two-stage sgRNA sequencing-library preparation roughly followed a published pooled CRISPR screening protocol ([Mathiowetz et al., 2023](https://www.cell.com/star-protocols/fulltext/S2666-1667(23)00159-4)). At D0 and the passage-4 endpoint (plus passage 3 in A549), genomic DNA was isolated from pelleted cells using the Blood and Cell Culture DNA Maxi Kit (Qiagen, #13362). To preserve $400\times$ representation during amplification, integrated sgRNA cassettes were amplified from 310 µg of genomic DNA per sample across 31 parallel 100-µL first-stage PCR reactions (10 µg DNA per reaction, 1× ExtremeTaq HiFi Mix [Azura Genomics], and 1 µM outer primers for 18 cycles), pooled, and dual-indexed in second-stage PCR reactions (10 µL pooled PCR1 template and 0.5 µM indexed primers for 20 cycles; primer sequences and cycling parameters in **Supplementary Methods S4.2.1**). Indexed amplicons were purified through two rounds of 2% agarose gel extraction (Monarch DNA Gel Extraction Kit, NEB #T1020S), verified by Qubit fluorometry and TapeStation D1000 electrophoresis, and sequenced by Azenta Life Sciences using 150-bp paired-end Illumina reads (targeting $1.2 \times 10^8$ reads per library with 25% PhiX spike-in).

### 4.2.3 Screen read processing, quality control, and differential-dependency scoring

#### 4.2.3.1 Read counting and screen quality control

Sequencing read quality was verified with FastQC v0.12.1 and MultiQC v1.19. Forward reads were trimmed of the first 22 5′-flanking bases and matched to the 20-nt Brunello sgRNA reference using `MAGeCK count` (v0.5.9.5; [Li et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-014-0554-4)). Library representation and sample concordance were evaluated using mapped read fractions, zero-count guide frequencies, Gini indices, pairwise $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM} + 1)$ Pearson and raw-count Spearman correlations, and principal-component analysis across variable guides.

To confirm that proliferation-essential genes depleted as expected from D0 to P4 in both genotypes, per-guide $\log_2$ fold changes between mean endpoint CPM and D0 CPM were calculated for guides with $\ge 50$ D0 reads (after masking 173 D0 PCR-over-represented outlier guides identified by $|Z| > 3$ in HT1080 D0 libraries; **Supplementary Methods S4.3.1**), averaged per gene, and evaluated by receiver-operating-characteristic (ROC) analysis against Achilles common-essential and non-essential reference gene lists. Cross-screen audits of globally undetected guides (zero reads across all libraries) and D0/P3 PCR-over-represented guides are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S4.3.2** and **Supp Fig** 4.6.

#### 4.2.3.2 Differential-dependency scoring (drugZ, MAGeCK, and IsogenicZ)

To identify genes whose knockout selectively impaired or enhanced fitness in Y-depleted cells relative to Y-retaining control cells at the P4 endpoint, we used `drugZ` ([Colic et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13073-019-0665-3)) as the primary differential-essentiality method across the 19,112 targeted genes. In the unpaired formulation, each endpoint sample is normalized by library size, and normalized counts for each sgRNA are averaged within the LOY and CTRL groups. In HT1080, each group comprised two outgrowth replicates. The pseudocount-adjusted $\log_2$ ratio of these arithmetic group means gives one fold change per guide. An empirical-Bayes standard deviation estimated from guides of comparable mean raw abundance in the control group converts each fold change into a guide-level $Z$ score. Guide scores are summed within each gene, divided by the square root of the number of contributing non-zero guide scores, and standardized across tested genes to obtain `normZ`. Each guide therefore contributes one score from the group-mean comparison. Negative `normZ` indicates stronger depletion/sensitization in LOY cells, and positive `normZ` indicates relative enrichment/resistance. One-sided normal-distribution $P$ values are adjusted by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure separately for each direction. Genes meeting directional $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$ constituted the primary high-confidence hits, and $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$ defined the broader exploratory candidate set. Reconstruction checks supporting the unpaired formulation and the limits of the original execution record are described in **Supplementary Methods S4.3.3**.

To verify that candidate calls did not depend on a single statistical framework or on starting guide representation at D0, we performed two supporting analyses on the same screen counts (**Supplementary Methods S4.3.3**):
1. **MAGeCK robust rank aggregation (RRA):** Endpoint counts were normalized to the 1,000 non-targeting control guides and scored at the gene level by `MAGeCK` RRA (`alphamedian` $\log_2$ fold change) to compare rank and FDR concordance with `drugZ` (**Supp Fig** 4.7a).
2. **Baseline-adjusted IsogenicZ:** To account for differences in starting guide abundance between the CTRL and LOY D0 libraries, endpoint counts were normalized to each genotype's D0 baseline using `IsogenicZ` (after excluding non-targeting controls, the 173 D0 outlier guides, and globally absent guides, or requiring $\ge 50$ D0 reads in sensitivity fits) before unpaired empirical-Bayes $Z$-score aggregation (**Supp Fig** 4.7b).

In the A549 screen, one control P4 library (`CTRL_P4_R1`) was unavailable following library preparation, so the primary P4 endpoint analysis used the same unpaired formulation to compare mean normalized guide counts from `LOY_P4_R1` and `LOY_P4_R2` against `CTRL_P4_R2` across 19,112 genes. Because A549 P3 libraries exhibited PCR jackpotting of a small subset of guides, P3 samples were excluded from the primary analysis and evaluated separately after masking the 342 top-count guides under both `drugZ` and D0-adjusted `IsogenicZ` (**Supplementary Methods S4.2.2**).

### 4.2.4 Pathway enrichment, co-dependency networks, multi-omic integration, and external screen comparisons

#### 4.2.4.1 Gene-set enrichment and over-representation analyses

To detect coordinated shifts across protein complexes and metabolic pathways without relying on a fixed significance cutoff, preranked gene-set enrichment analysis was performed with `fgsea` (v1.36.2; 10,000 permutations, gene-set sizes 15–500) on the 18,960 HGNC-harmonized genes ranked by $-\mathrm{normZ}$ (so that positive normalized enrichment scores [NES] denote greater depletion/dependency in LOY cells). Rankings from HT1080 `drugZ`, HT1080 D0-adjusted `IsogenicZ` (with and without removal of 1,526 DepMap common-essential genes), and A549 `drugZ` were tested against the 50 MSigDB Hallmark gene sets and the combined 5,880-set collection spanning Hallmark, Reactome, GO:BP, and GO:CC (MSigDB v2026.1.Hs), with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across tested sets per ranking. Exploratory over-representation of the discrete HT1080 `drugZ` ($n = 48$) and `IsogenicZ` ($n = 45$) depleted candidate sets ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$) was evaluated with Metascape against the tested-gene universe (**Supplementary Methods S4.4.1**).

#### 4.2.4.2 STRING physical/functional and DepMap co-dependency networks

To organize the 57 HT1080 `drugZ` candidates ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$: 48 sensitizers and 9 resistors) into physical complexes and functional modules, we constructed two complementary networks (**Supplementary Methods S4.4.1**): (1) curated and predicted protein–protein associations from STRING v12.5 (human combined score $\ge 0.4$), and (2) genetic co-essentiality across 1,186 cancer cell lines in DepMap 25Q3 (pairwise Pearson correlations of Chronos dependency profiles across $\ge 200$ shared models, retaining edges with $|r| > 0.2$). Functional communities were identified by weighted Louvain modularity optimization (`NetworkX` v3.5) on positive DepMap co-dependency edges ($r > 0.2$) and displayed on a shared spring-embedded layout. To confirm that co-dependency clustering among SAGA complex subunits was not driven by tissue lineage or sex, pairwise Chronos correlations across all 20 SAGA subunits were also computed from residuals after regressing out Oncotree lineage and sex.

#### 4.2.4.3 Screen integration with gene expression, chromatin states and rescue profiles induced by LOY

To test whether HT1080 differential dependencies reflected direct transcriptional silencing of the candidate genes themselves or altered activity of their downstream regulatory programs, we integrated the HT1080 screen results with the Chapter 3 baseline HT1080 RNA-seq ($n = 25,526$ genes across batches 1 and 2), promoter ATAC-seq ($\mathrm{TSS} \pm 2\text{ kb}$), and *UTY*/*KDM5D* lentiviral rescue RNA-seq datasets (**Supplementary Methods S4.4.1**). At the gene level, candidate hits ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$) and curated chromatin/metabolic complex members were evaluated against the Chapter 3 baseline differential-expression criteria ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$, $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}| \ge 1$, and concordant direction across batches 1 and 2) and median promoter accessibility changes. At the pathway level, representative gene sets spanning the major screen themes (SAGA, SWI/SNF, sterol biosynthesis, MYC targets, proliferation, mitochondrial translation, and oxidative phosphorylation) were compared across the CRISPR screen, baseline RNA-seq, promoter ATAC-seq, and *UTY*/*KDM5D* rescue RNA-seq enrichment analyses described in Chapter 3 (**Supplementary Methods S4.4.1**).

#### 4.2.4.4 Comparison with external isogenic CRISPR screens and BioGRID ORCS

To evaluate whether HT1080 candidate hits represent vulnerabilities specific to Y loss or recurrent hits across general isogenic CRISPR screens, we reprocessed raw guide counts from 27 published TKOv3 isogenic knockout, knock-in, and chromosome-arm-loss screens (15 contrasts from [Desjardins et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117961), including 13q and 18q arm losses, and 12 batch-matched tumour-suppressor knockouts in HEK293A from [Feng et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9106303/); **Supplementary Methods S4.4.2**). Each contrast was scored by unpaired endpoint `drugZ` (and T0-adjusted `IsogenicZ` sensitivity) across 17,737 shared HGNC-harmonized genes, and subjected to the same 5,880-set `fgsea` pipeline. To test whether the 48 HT1080 depleted candidates ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$) recurred across the 27 external isogenic contrasts and in BioGRID ORCS v2.0.18 (890 baseline and 24 genotype-conditioned human Cas9 screens) more often than expected by chance, observed overlap counts were compared against 10,000 random 48-gene sets matched for the proportion of DepMap common-essential genes (**Supplementary Methods S4.4.2**).

### 4.2.5 Two-colour GFP/mCherry competitive growth assay and targeted gene perturbation

#### 4.2.5.1 Reporter-labelled populations, reciprocal design, and spectral flow cytometry

To validate individual candidate dependencies under shared culture conditions that control for media composition, confluence, and passaging variation while avoiding the sensitivity of monoculture proliferation assays to initial cell-counting and seeding variance (since each mixture is normalized directly to its measured day-0 baseline ratio), we developed a fluorescence-based co-culture competitive growth assay pairing Y-depleted (LOY) and Y-retaining (CTRL) HT1080 cells stably transduced with lentiviral GFP or mCherry reporters. Every competition was performed in reciprocal reporter orientations (`LOY–GFP + CTRL–mCherry` and `LOY–mCherry + CTRL–GFP`) so that genotype-dependent fitness effects could be separated from fluorophore-intrinsic growth differences. Assay backgrounds comprised the original heterogeneous HT1080 populations (used for *SREBF1* and *TADA2B*) and pools of six ddPCR-verified Y-depleted or Y-intact single-cell clones (used for wild-type/non-targeting control and *DDX3X*-targeted arms, as well as initial ratio-validation mixtures; **Supplementary Methods S4.5.1**).

Flow-cytometry acquisitions were performed on a BD FACSDiscover A8 spectral cytometer. After sequential polygon gating on scatter and singlets (`FACSChorus`), GFP, mCherry, and endogenous autofluorescence signals were unmixed with `SpectralFX` using single-colour and unstained HT1080 reference controls (`mcherry_GFP_matrix`) and transformed as $\operatorname{arcsinh}(\text{intensity}/1000)$. After excluding double-negative unlabelled events, singlets were assigned to GFP or mCherry classes using an equal-prior two-class linear discriminant analysis (LDA) classifier trained on pooled single-colour controls across genotypes (**Supplementary Methods S4.5.2**). Assay linearity and technical repeatability were validated across defined 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 90% LOY:CTRL mixtures in both reporter orientations, and compared against earlier conventional flow-cytometry acquisitions on a Beckman Coulter CytoFLEX S (**Supplementary Methods S4.5.1–S4.5.2**).

#### 4.2.5.2 Targeted CRISPR–Cas9 RNP perturbation and competition time-course analysis

For targeted validation, *DDX3X* (positive control), *TADA2B*, *TAF6L*, and *SREBF1* were disrupted in matched LOY and CTRL cells in the initial competition series (with additional knockouts targeting *KIF2A*, *MED12*, and *TIMM8A* underway) using pools of three synthetic sgRNAs per gene (Synthego, designed independently of the Brunello library guides) complexed with SpCas9 2NLS nuclease (180 pmol total sgRNA and 20 pmol Cas9 in 25 µL SF Nucleofector solution with Supplement 1) and delivered into $1.5 \times 10^5$ cells via Lonza 4D-Nucleofector program CM-130 alongside non-targeting RNP controls. Target-site cleavage was verified qualitatively by genomic PCR and agarose gel electrophoresis across the edited loci (**Supplementary Methods S4.5.1**).

For competitive growth assays, viable LOY and CTRL cells carrying the same perturbation were mixed at approximately 1:1 in triplicate wells per reporter orientation (three post-split cultures per orientation, sampled at 10,000 events per well on the BD FACSDiscover A8 at baseline [D0] and passaged at a 1:10 split every 3–5 days over a planned 28-day time course). Relative fitness of LOY versus CTRL cells in culture $i$ at timepoint $t$ was quantified by the change in log odds of the LOY fraction ($p_{i,t}$) relative to its measured starting mixture baseline ($p_{i,0}$):

$$
\Delta_{i,t} = \ln\left(\frac{p_{i,t}}{1 - p_{i,t}}\right) - \ln\left(\frac{p_{i,0}}{1 - p_{i,0}}\right),
$$

where $\exp(\Delta_{i,t})$ represents the fold change in the $\mathrm{LOY}:\mathrm{CTRL}$ cell ratio from D0. Day-4 (D4) processing, density-valley gating for the unlabelled subpopulation in *TADA2B*, and gating sensitivity checks are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S4.5.2**.
