# Chapter 4 — Supplementary Materials and Methods

## S4.1 Extended public-data dependency, drug-sensitivity, and power-simulation methods

### S4.1.1 Alternative Y-status definitions and random-forest models

In the DepMap 26Q1 Y-status classification (Section 4.2.1.1), sensitivity analyses evaluated RNA-only, DNA-only, and strictly RNA–DNA concordant calls, omitted the gene-breadth requirement, shifted the RNA expression thresholds by $\pm 20\%$ (including 95th-percentile female reference thresholds), excluded models with Y-chromosome copy gains or elevated *XIST* expression, or restricted Y-positive controls to single-copy Y models.

For Boruta random-forest feature selection, models were fitted in `ranger` (500 trees per iteration, 100 iterations) across 6,037 selectively dependent genes (dependency probability $\ge 0.5$ in at least five models and $< 0.5$ in at least five models, with complete measurements across the 568-model cohort). Shadow features were generated by permuting each feature within Oncotree lineage while preserving lineage and CRISPR library covariates (`LibraryKY` and `LibraryHumagneCD`). Features exceeding the maximum shadow importance were tallied across iterations and confirmed by one-sided binomial test ($P_{\mathrm{Bonferroni}} < 0.01$ against a hit probability of 0.5). Null permutation controls repeated the procedure three times after shuffling Y-status labels within each lineage.

To evaluate how strongly Y-chromosome status and Y-gene expression predict the four established X-linked paralog dependencies (*DDX3X*, *EIF1AX*, *RPS4X*, and *ZFX*, plus a standardized composite score), four cumulative random-forest models (500 trees) were evaluated using ten-fold donor-grouped cross-validation repeated three times (**Supp Fig** 4.1c): (1) lineage and screening library covariates; (2) addition of categorical Y status (including unresolved models); (3) addition of $\log_2(\mathrm{TPM} + 1)$ expression of the seven core Y-linked genes; and (4) addition of the 1,000 non-Y genes with the strongest absolute correlation with the target dependency score within each training fold (restricted to genes with mean $\log_2(\mathrm{TPM} + 1) \ge 1$ and $\mathrm{SD} \ge 0.25$).

### S4.1.2 Replication in Sanger and DepMap NextGen organoid and neurosphere cohorts

In the Sanger malignant-organoid dataset ($n = 95$ male models; *EIF1AX* not present in the library), published scaled $\log_2$ fold-change dependency scores were analyzed with `limma` adjusting for cancer lineage, library version (`minLib` versus `v1_1`), and microsatellite-instability status. In the DepMap NextGen organoid and neurosphere cohort ($n = 65$ male models: 27 Y-negative and 38 Y-positive), significance across the four established genes was assessed by permutation testing with Y-status labels shuffled within cancer-lineage and screening-format strata, followed by Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the four genes. For cross-dataset comparison plots, standardized effects were computed as signed $Z$ statistics derived from two-sided model $P$ values multiplied by the standard error of the Y-status coefficient assuming unit residual variance.

### S4.1.3 Context-stratified genetic dependency models and empirical power simulations

Context-stratified analyses evaluated 6,089 selective non-MSY genes measured in at least 90% of the 568-model male CRISPR cohort. Chronos scores were rank-normalized per gene across the full cohort before subsetting, and `limma` models were fitted with robust empirical-Bayes moderation; separate untransformed fits provided effect sizes in raw Chronos units. Calling $Y = 1$ for Y-negative and $Y = 0$ for Y-positive models, $L$ for Oncotree lineage, and $B$ for library indicators (`LibraryKY`, `LibraryHumagneCD`), models required at least three residual degrees of freedom:

1. **Within-cancer subsets:** Across Oncotree lineages (14 eligible), primary diseases (11 eligible), and subtypes (7 eligible) with at least five models per Y group, $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim L + B + Y$ was tested with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across 6,089 genes within each subset.
2. **Driver-mutated subsets across cancers:** Across 47 driver genes mutated (DepMap `Hotspot`, `OncogeneHighImpact`, `TumorSuppressorHighImpact`, or `HessDriver` flags) in at least 20 male models with at least five models in each $2 \times 2$ ($\text{Y status} \times \text{driver status}$) cell, within-driver associations used $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim L + B + Y$ and driver-interaction tests across all models used $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim L + B + Y + D + Y:D$. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across all $6,089 \times 47$ tests within each test family; driver specificity required both within-driver and interaction $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$. Binary copy-number amplification (relative ratio $\ge 2.0$), deep deletion ($\le 0.25$), and molecular-subtype contexts were analyzed analogously.
3. **Continuous genomic contexts:** For chromosomal instability (CIN), ploidy, aneuploidy, and loss-of-heterozygosity fraction (requiring $\ge 20$ models per Y group and standardized to unit variance as $S$), interaction models fitted $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim L + B + Y + S + Y:S$. Together, the binary and continuous pan-cancer context classes encompassed 86 contexts and 523,654 interaction tests.
4. **Combined cancer-type and driver-alteration subsets:** Across 68 eligible cancer-by-driver strata ($\ge 5$ altered models per Y group across 22 cancer types and 14 drivers), joint models fitted $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim B + D + Y_{\mathrm{unaltered}} + Y_{\mathrm{alt}}$ (414,052 within-altered tests), and in the 24 strata with $\ge 5$ models in all four cells, interaction models fitted $\mathrm{Chronos}^{\mathrm{rank}}_g \sim B + D + Y + Y:D$ (146,136 interaction tests).

To evaluate statistical detection limits across the 30 unique eligible cancer groups, uniform-shift simulations selected 500 fully measured selective autosomal/X-linked genes (excluding the four pan-cancer hits and X–Y gametologs), subtracted a fixed shift (in Chronos or residual-SD units) from all LOY lines within the group while preserving observed inter-line variance, rank-normalized the resulting profile, and tested it against the full 6,089-gene discovery family at within-group $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$. Benchmark transfer simulations estimated the observed effect size and residual variance of 25 established synthetic-lethal, oncogene-addiction, and paralog dependencies in DepMap and resampled 400 synthetic profiles per benchmark and cancer group matching that group's sample sizes and lineage composition.

### S4.1.4 Extended drug-sensitivity models

Pan-cancer drug-response analyses across CTRPv2, GDSC1, GDSC2, and four PRISM Repurposing datasets (Primary [REP.PRIMARY], 1M [REP.1M], 300 [REP.300], and Secondary; 6,413 compound–dataset comparisons with $\ge 10$ models per Y group; PRISM primary compounds additionally required $\log_2\text{ fold change} \le -1$ in $\ge 5$ models) used ordinary least squares with lineage and PRISM batch covariates and donor-clustered HC3 standard errors, corrected by Benjamini–Hochberg within each dataset. For the 352 compounds assayed in at least two dataset families, within-source $Z$-standardized responses were pooled using linear mixed-effects models with fixed lineage, dataset, and Y-status terms and a random `ModelID` intercept (1-d.f. likelihood-ratio test, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected across 352 compounds). Mechanism-of-action (218 classes) and target-gene (385 classes) enrichments for classes with $\ge 5$ compounds averaged lineage-adjusted compound $t$ statistics and derived two-sided empirical $P$ values from 2,000 within-lineage Y-label permutations linked by `ModelID`. Within-subset drug comparisons ($\ge 5$ measured models per Y group inside and outside the subset; 495,079 tests across 121 subsets in eight context classes) used rank-normalized responses ($z_d \sim L + B + Y$) with Benjamini–Hochberg correction within each context class, paired with analytical noncentral-$t$ minimum detectable effect calculations for 80% power at $\alpha = 0.05$ and Bonferroni-equivalent dataset thresholds.

## S4.2 Extended CRISPR screen protocols and library-preparation records

### S4.2.1 Brunello library transduction and PCR primer sequences

In both HT1080 and A549 screens (Section 4.2.2), lentiviral uptake was titrated in six-well plates ($1.0 \times 10^5$ cells per well, 5–30 µL virus series, 5 µg/mL puromycin for 3 days) against uninfected controls. Large-scale transductions used $3.0 \times 10^8$ cells per genotype with 8 mL of Addgene #73179-LV Brunello library virus supplemented with 5 µL/mL Pluronic F-108 stock and 10 µL/mL polybrene stock. First-stage PCR amplification of integrated sgRNA cassettes from genomic DNA (31 reactions $\times 10\ \mu\text{g}$ DNA per sample; 310 µg total) used the following outer primers:

| PCR1 primer | Sequence (5′–3′) |
|---|---|
| Forward | `TCATATGCTTACCGTAACTTGAAA` |
| Reverse | `AACTTCTCGGGGACTGTGG` |

### S4.2.2 A549 sample inventory, P4 primary reconstruction, and P3 outlier-trimmed sensitivity

In the A549 screen, DNA was collected at D0 (two technical libraries per genotype, `D0_1` and `D0_2`), passage 3 (P3), and passage 4 (P4). Preparation of `CTRL_P4_R1` was unavailable following PCR/library construction, leaving `CTRL_P4_R2`, `LOY_P4_R1`, and `LOY_P4_R2` for the primary P4 endpoint contrast (`A549_P4_LOY_vs_CTRL.txt`, 19,112 biological genes). An unpaired `drugZ` reconstruction using ten-million-count scaling, pseudocount 5, empirical-Bayes half-window 500, and removal of non-targeting controls before normalization reproduced the historical P4 gene scores to within $|\Delta\mathrm{normZ}| \le 0.01$ and recovered the identical 19 depleted genes at $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$.

Because the A549 P3 libraries exhibited severe read domination by a small subset of guides, P3 samples were excluded from the primary analysis and evaluated in a dedicated sensitivity analysis. Based on the experimental culture records, the library originally labelled `CTRL_P3_R1` was confirmed to be a Y-depleted culture and reassigned to LOY for the P3 sensitivity analysis (`CTRL_P3_R2` versus `LOY_P3_R1`, `LOY_P3_R2`, and the reassigned culture). To mitigate jackpotted guide dominance without gene-specific selection, a fixed 342-guide mask (the union of the 250 highest-count guides in each of the four P3 libraries: 272 targeting guides and 70 non-targeting controls, removing all 4 guides for 30 genes) was applied across all D0, P3, and P4 libraries. Samples were normalized by median-ratio scaling across the 925 retained non-targeting guides with $\ge 50$ reads in all four D0 libraries relative to the mean pooled D0 CPM reference, and scored by unpaired `drugZ` and baseline-adjusted `IsogenicZ` ($\ge 50$ D0 reads, 74,629 targeting guides across 19,082 genes).

## S4.3 Screen quality-control audits and baseline-adjustment parameters

### S4.3.1 D0 PCR-jackpotting diagnosis and baseline outlier mask

In HT1080, the 100 highest-abundance guides in the D0 libraries accounted for 71.0% of mapped reads across the two D0 libraries but only 0.124% of reads across the four P4 endpoint libraries, where their median abundance matched the rest of the library (10.8 versus 10.2 CPM). For D0-dependent quality-control and baseline-adjusted `IsogenicZ` analyses, a fixed baseline outlier mask excluded 173 guides (0.22% of the library) whose $\log_2(\mathrm{CPM} + 1)$ exceeded $|Z| > 3$ relative to the within-sample mean and standard deviation in either D0 library. For D0-to-endpoint essential-gene ROC analyses (**Fig** 4.2c; **Supp Fig** 4.5g), CPM was recalculated after removing the 173-guide mask, guides were required to have $\ge 50$ raw D0 reads within genotype, and gene-level mean $\log_2[(\text{mean endpoint CPM} + 1)/(\text{D0 CPM} + 1)]$ was evaluated against Achilles common-essential and non-essential reference gene lists.

### S4.3.2 Cross-screen guide non-detection and sequence-motif audits

Across the retained HT1080 (six D0/P4) and A549 (seven D0/P4) libraries, 223 of 77,441 Brunello guides (0.288%) had zero reads in every library of both screens (236 undetected throughout HT1080 and 307 throughout A549; **Supp Fig** 4.6a), including single guides targeting *DDX3X* (`DDX3X_4640`), *MED12*, and *CDK9*. Among the 223 shared zero-read guides, 196 (87.9%) began with a 5′ `TCTC` or `AGACG` sequence prefix, compared with 221 of 77,121 detected guides (0.287%; an exploratory frequency ratio of $307\times$; **Supp Fig** 4.6b).

Comparison of the 173 HT1080 D0 outlier guides with the 272 targeting guides in the A549 P3 exclusion mask revealed 172 shared guides spanning 70 genes (including 30 genes with all four guides present) whose mean CPM rankings correlated at Spearman $\rho = 0.997$ between HT1080 D0 and A549 P3 (**Supp Fig** 4.6c,d). Exhaustive one-sided hypergeometric testing across 32,880 1–6-nt prefixes, suffixes, internal words, and position-specific nucleotide features identified no motif passing Benjamini–Hochberg $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$ (minimum $\mathrm{FDR} = 0.273$; **Supp Fig** 4.6e), and these diagnostic audits did not alter the primary candidate calls.

### S4.3.3 drugZ reconstruction and IsogenicZ sensitivity branches

The original command and software revision that generated the primary HT1080 `drugZ` output were unavailable. A reconstruction using the saved endpoint counts and unpaired scoring (`unpaired=True`, corresponding to `-unpaired` in the local implementation), ten-million-count scaling, fold-change pseudocount 5 and empirical-Bayes half-window 500 closely reproduced the saved gene scores (Pearson $r = 0.999995$; maximum absolute `normZ` difference <0.0156). The numbers of contributing guide scores matched exactly, and depleted and enriched hit sets were identical at both $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.05$ and $\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$. This supports the unpaired formulation but does not recover the original invocation or establish an exact numerical reproduction. The historical primary scores and FDRs were retained.

Baseline-adjusted differential essentiality was evaluated using `IsogenicZ` (https://github.com/kdelint/IsogenicZ, commit `e9327aba6aeff0fe5c88f1b94e0dabed914f01a7`). In the primary displayed HT1080 `IsogenicZ` branch (`mask173_detected`), non-targeting controls, the 173 D0 outlier guides, and 235 targeting guides with zero reads across all six libraries were excluded prior to normalization (retaining 76,033 targeting guides across 19,082 genes) so that globally absent guides did not receive non-zero scores from pseudocount scaling. Each sample was scaled to $10^7$ counts with a 0.1-per-guide pseudocount, multiplied by the summed normalized CTRL and LOY D0 abundances and divided by its own genotype's normalized D0 abundance, rescaled to $10^7$, and scored by unpaired group-mean `IsogenicZ` (fold-change pseudocount 5, empirical-Bayes half-window 500, ordered by mean raw CTRL endpoint abundance). Sensitivity branches retained all targeting guides, excluded only the 173-guide mask, or required $\ge 50$ raw reads in both D0 libraries (retaining 46,767 guides across 17,883 genes).

## S4.4 Network, multi-omic integration, and external screen details

### S4.4.1 Gene-symbol harmonization, GSEA, and network layout parameters

For preranked `fgsea` (v1.36.2; `gseaParam = 1`, set sizes 15–500, `eps = 0`, `nPermSimple = 10,000`, seed `20261021`), Brunello library symbols were updated to current HGNC symbols via `org.Hs.eg.db` alias mapping, removing ambiguous or colliding mappings to leave 18,960 ranked genes per screen. Sensitivity rankings removed 1,526 DepMap CRISPR common-essential genes prior to enrichment. Metascape over-representation analyses (database v3.5) evaluated the 48-gene `drugZ` and 45-gene `IsogenicZ` depleted sets ($\mathrm{FDR} < 0.25$) against their respective tested-gene backgrounds using full-list hypergeometric $P$ values and Benjamini–Hochberg $q$ values.

In the 57-gene HT1080 network analysis (Section 4.2.4.2), *ZCCHC16* mapped to STRING v12.5 symbol *RTL4* and lacked a DepMap Chronos profile, leaving 56 profiles for pairwise correlation heatmaps. A SAGA-focused sensitivity regressed DepMap 25Q3 Chronos scores across 1,186 models on Oncotree lineage and recorded sex for 20 SAGA subunits and four alternative members before computing residual Pearson correlations. Shared network node coordinates were generated by NetworkX v3.5 spring layout (`k = 0.65`, 700 iterations, seed `17`) from combined edge weights ($4r$ for positive DepMap edges, $0.15$ for negative edges, plus $0.35 \times \text{STRING score}$), followed by deterministic label-repulsion adjustment.

### S4.4.2 External isogenic screen register and BioGRID ORCS curation

The 27 external TKOv3 isogenic contrasts (Section 4.2.4.4) comprised 15 genetic contrasts from Desjardins et al. (2026; Mendeley Data `10.17632/k6wm46g4tw.1`, excluding compound-treatment screens and orienting *KEAP1* as mutant parental A549 versus wild-type restored clone) and 12 batch-matched tumour-suppressor knockouts in HEK293A from Feng et al. (2022; figshare `19398332`, excluding *TP53BP1* which lacked a batch-matched wild-type control). After HGNC symbol harmonization, 17,737 genes were shared across HT1080 and all 27 external contrasts. In BioGRID ORCS v2.0.18 (1,952 human screens), hit fractions were compiled across 890 baseline negative-selection Cas9 time-course screens (requiring $\ge 100$ measuring screens to accommodate X-linked genes) and 24 genotype-conditioned screens (requiring $\ge 12$ measuring screens).

## S4.5 Extended flow-cytometry development and gating sensitivity analyses

### S4.5.1 Clonal pool derivation and initial CytoFLEX S development

In the *DDX3X*-targeted and wild-type (non-targeting RNP) clonal pool backgrounds (Section 4.2.5.1), single-cell colonies were isolated from 10-cm dishes using cloning rings, screened by ddPCR for *RPS4Y1* (Yp), *KDM5D* (Yq), and *AMELY*/*AMELX*, and pooled as six Y-depleted clones with no detectable Y signal (`LOY1`, `LOY3`, `LOY4`, `LOY5`, `LOY7`, `LOY8`) and six Y-intact clones with approximately one Y copy per cell (`CTRL1`, `CTRL3`, `CTRL4`, `CTRL6`, `CTRL7`, `CTRL8`) before lentiviral GFP or mCherry transduction. Initial detector-gain, spillover, and gating checks were conducted on a Beckman Coulter CytoFLEX S across two preliminary calibration runs and retained solely as method-development records.

### S4.5.2 Spectral unmixing, saturation flags, and D4 label and gating audits

On the BD FACSDiscover A8 (Section 4.2.5.2), `FACSChorus` Scatter, SSC Singlets, and FSC Singlets polygon gates were applied sequentially, and `SpectralFX` unmixed `eGFP-A`, `mCherry-A`, and `Autofluorescence_001-A` using the stored `mcherry_GFP_matrix` reference. In the primary D0 baseline and D4 competition recordings, low-reporter double-negative events ($\operatorname{arcsinh}(\text{eGFP-A}/1000) < 6$ and $\operatorname{arcsinh}(\text{mCherry-A}/1000) < 6.5$) were excluded prior to applying the 16-control pooled equal-prior linear discriminant analysis (LDA) classifier. Because the *TADA2B*-targeted heterogeneous mixture contained an unlabelled subpopulation near the standard mCherry threshold whose position shifted slightly between D0 and D4, a day-specific density-valley threshold between the unlabelled (5.0–7.4) and mCherry-positive (7.6–9.0) peaks was applied to *TADA2B* (6.95 at D0 and 7.55 at D4), with the fixed 6.5 threshold retained as a sensitivity check.

At D4, cell-intrinsic light-loss, imaging-SSC, and cell-size features trained on known-genotype WT competition wells confirmed a plate-row swap between the same-genotype `CTRL/CTRL` and `LOY/LOY` control blocks (`C01–C03` and `C04–C06`), which was corrected in the sample metadata map without altering competition mixture wells. Sensitivity analyses across all arms confirmed that relative log-odds shifts ($\Delta_{i,\mathrm{D4}}$) were robust to strict low-reporter exclusion, standard fixed gating, removal of detector saturation flags, and $\pm 0.5$-unit shifts in the LDA decision boundary.
