# Chapter 3 — Materials and Methods

## 3.2 Materials and Methods

All code and analysis workflows used in this chapter will be made publicly available at https://github.com/michaelcvermeulen/loy-synthetic-lethal. Extended sample inventories, assay validation checks, sequencing quality-control summaries (**Supp Fig** 3.1–3.3), sensitivity analyses, and study-specific external dataset processing are provided in **Supplementary Methods S3.1–S3.4** (**Appendix 2**).

### 3.2.1 Guide design and target annotation

Both strands of HG002 T2T-Y (CP086569.2) were scanned in R using 23-nucleotide windows to identify 20-nucleotide protospacers adjacent to NGG PAMs ([Rhie et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06457-y)). Exact matches were counted across both orientations using Biostrings v2.78.0. Candidates required at least ten exact Y matches and no exact matches to T2T-CHM13 autosomes, chromosome X or mitochondrial DNA. The final shortlist excluded guides with recorded non-Y matches containing one or two mismatches.

Selection considered exact Y-match count and the number of 1-Mb bins containing targets, yielding CN, CP, Q53, P47 and P51. Exact reference matches were mapped with strand-appropriate NGG PAMs and intersected with T2T-CHM13v2.0 Cen/Sat v2.1 and Composite Repeats 2022DEC annotations to identify DYZ3 α-satellite, HSat3 A6/DYZ1 and TSPY-associated repeats. Counts represent PAM-compatible reference sites.

**Y-targeting guide sequences.** Sequences are shown 5′–3′ without the PAM.

| Guide | Protospacer sequence |

| CN | ATGATAGGTTGAACTCCCGG |
| CP | ACTATCTTCGCATGAGAACC |
| Q53 | TCGCCAGGAATTGATGTGAA |
| P47 | TTCGCGCGCAGTCCCTTAGG |
| P51 | ATTAAGGCCCACCTGAGTAA |

### 3.2.2 Generation and genomic characterization of Y-depleted populations

#### 3.2.2.1 Cell culture and sample collection

HT1080 (CCL-121), A549 (CCL-185) and NCI-H2452 (CRL-5946; hereafter H2452) cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). DU145 cells were a gift from the Dr. Susan Cole laboratory, and KP4 cells were obtained from Leapfrog Bio. HT1080, A549 and KP4 cells were maintained in DMEM (Wisent), DU145 cells in RPMI-1640 (Wisent), and H2452 cells in RPMI (Wisent), all supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS (Corning) and 1% (v/v) antibiotic–antimycotic (Wisent) at 37 °C in 5% CO₂. Cells were passaged at 1:10 (1:5 for H2452) using trypsin (Wisent) at 80–100% confluence.

Cell-line identity was verified against CCLE and GEO reference expression profiles, by STR authentication at The Centre for Applied Genomics (TCAG) for DU145, A549 and HT1080, and by routine mycoplasma testing. For DNA and RNA extraction, cells were washed in PBS, pelleted for 5 min at 4 °C and stored at −80 °C. Across baseline, parental and UTY/KDM5D rescue experiments, molecular profiling encompassed 44 unique RNA-seq libraries, six HT1080 ATAC-seq libraries and baseline lpWGS profiles; the complete sample inventory and sampling relationships across assays are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S3.1.1** and the [RNA-seq sample table](../../tables/chapter3/molecular_samples/rna_library_map.csv).

#### 3.2.2.2 RNP delivery, pharmacological modulation and RT-qPCR screening

Chemically modified sgRNAs and purified SpCas9 (Synthego) were assembled at 180 pmol total sgRNA and 20 pmol Cas9 per reaction (9:1) in 25 µL SF Cell Line Nucleofector solution with Supplement 1 for 10 min at room temperature. Guides were delivered individually or in equimolar pools. Approximately 150,000 cells in 5 µL Nucleofector solution were added to each RNP mixture (30 µL total) and nucleofected using the Lonza 4D-Nucleofector X Unit with the SF Cell Line 4D-Nucleofector X Kit S (V4XC-2032), using program FF-113 for HT1080, program CM-130 for A549 ([Lonza protocol](https://bioscience.lonza.com/lonza_bs/US/en/download/product/asset/39472)), and program EN-120 for DU145. Parallel control populations received a Synthego non-targeting control sgRNA (sgCTRL). Cells recovered in 12-well plates in 1 mL complete medium and were expanded as pooled populations. HT1080 and A549 received CP or Q53 individually or combined (including two successive CP + Q53 nucleofections), and DU145 received CP + Q53 + P47 once or twice (the twice-nucleofected population supplied the RNA-seq comparison).

To test pharmacological modulation during DU145 Y targeting, cells nucleofected with CP + Q53 + P47 were compared across six conditions: DMSO vehicle (0.1% v/v final), KU-55933 alone, panobinostat at 30 or 75 nM, and KU-55933 combined with each panobinostat concentration. Panobinostat (Selleck Chemicals, S1030) was applied for 24 h before nucleofection and removed at harvest. KU-55933 (Selleck Chemicals, S1092) at 10 µM was added 4 h after nucleofection and maintained for 16 h ([Celli et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13578-024-01198-5)), after which cells were washed with PBS and returned to drug-free medium.

For initial guide screening in HT1080, RNA was collected approximately four passages after individual nucleofection of CN, CP, Q53, P47, P51 or sgCTRL using the Monarch Total RNA Miniprep Kit (NEB, T2010), and cDNA was synthesized with the ProtoScript II First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (NEB, E6560). RT-qPCR used PowerUp SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, A25742) with *GAPDH* normalization. Relative expression of *EIF1AY*, *KDM5D* and *RPS4Y1* was quantified by $2^{-\Delta\Delta\mathrm{Ct}}$ relative to *GAPDH* and sgCTRL across three technical wells per run in two assay runs (one run for CN).

#### 3.2.2.3 ddPCR assessment of Y content

Genomic DNA was extracted using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, 69504), quantified by NanoDrop spectrophotometry or Qubit dsDNA fluorometry, and digested (1 µg DNA, 20 U HindIII [NEB, R0104S], 1× NEBuffer r2.1 in 50 µL at 37 °C for 1 h). Standard 20-µL ddPCR reactions contained 30 ng digested DNA, 10 µL 2× ddPCR Supermix for Probes (No dUTP; Bio-Rad), and 1× assay probes. The *AMELY*/*AMELX* assay (Applied Biosystems, C_990000001_10) used a shared primer pair and FAM/VIC probes spanning a 6-bp difference ([Danielsson et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-019-0533-z)). *DDX3Y* (Applied Biosystems, Hs02560117_cn), *KDM5D* and *RPS4Y1* were assayed separately in duplex with *RNaseP* using FAM target and HEX reference probes.

Droplets were generated on a QX200 Droplet Generator (Bio-Rad), sealed at 180 °C for 5 s, cycled (95 °C for 10 min; 40 cycles of 94 °C for 30 s and 60 °C for 1 min; 98 °C for 10 min; 2 °C/s ramp), and read on a QX200 Droplet Reader with QX Manager v2.3. Wells with fewer than 10,000 accepted droplets were excluded. Estimated Y copies per cell were calculated from target/reference ratios using reference copy numbers of one *AMELX* and two *RNaseP* copies for HT1080, and two *AMELX* and three *RNaseP* copies for A549. Validation of assay linearity across KP4/H2452 mixtures and descriptive comparison rules across lines are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S3.1.2**.

#### 3.2.2.4 Low-pass WGS and regional copy-number assessment

Genomic DNA was sequenced by GENEWIZ/Azenta on an Illumina NovaSeq platform (150-bp paired-end reads, ~1.5 Gb per sample). Reads were trimmed with fastp (removing the first 15 bases per mate, auto-detecting adapters and discarding reads <50 bp), aligned to the T2T-CHM13v2.0 analysis set (including HG002 chrY) using BWA-MEM2 v2.2.1, and sorted with samtools v1.23.1. Whole-Y depth relative to length-weighted autosomal depth was summarized with mosdepth.

Regional copy-number profiles were generated in custom T2T-CHM13v2.0 100-kb bins across autosomes and sex chromosomes using QDNAseq in paired-end mode with GC and mappability correction, median normalization, smoothing and circular binary segmentation on square-root-transformed values. Genome-wide displays used segmented $\log_2$ relative copy number, whereas Y-region summaries used median back-transformed, unsegmented corrected values within distal Yp (2.46–4.80 Mb), proximal Yp (6.00–10.00 Mb), male-specific Yp (2.458–10.566 Mb) and euchromatic Yq (10.883–27.450 Mb). Bin filtering settings and DU145 multi-profile arm-loss recurrence summaries are provided in **Supplementary Methods S3.1.3**.

### 3.2.3 Transcriptional and chromatin characterization

#### 3.2.3.1 RNA sequencing, alignment and quantification

Frozen cell pellets ($\ge 1 \times 10^6$ cells) were processed by GENEWIZ/Azenta using the Standard RNA-Seq service (poly(A)-selected, unstranded libraries, Illumina NovaSeq 150-bp paired-end reads, targeting 30 million read pairs per library). Read quality was assessed with FastQC v0.12.1 and trimmed using fastp v1.3.3 (adapter auto-detection, polyG trimming, Phred $\ge 15$ and length $\ge 15$ bp). Reads were aligned to GRCh38 using STAR in two-pass mode (--twopassMode Basic) with the GENCODE v49 annotation, and exon counts grouped by gene were quantified with featureCounts v2.1.1. Comprehensive quality-control metrics across all 44 libraries (MultiQC v1.34, Qualimap v2.3 and Picard CollectRnaSeqMetrics v3.1.0) are presented in **Supplementary Methods S3.2.1** and **Supp Fig** 3.1–3.2.

#### 3.2.3.2 Baseline RNA-seq analysis

Baseline RNA-seq used edgeR filtering and TMM normalization separately by cell line, followed by limma-voom with robust empirical Bayes moderation. Contrasts tested Y-depleted minus sgCTRL with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across tested genes. For HT1080, the established sgCTRL and Y-depleted populations were profiled across two independent RNA-seq collection batches separated by nine months: **batch 1** (collected in August 2025) comprised three successive passage collections per condition alongside three parental HT1080 libraries (nine libraries total), and **batch 2** (collected in May 2026) comprised three further matched passage collections of the same established sgCTRL and Y-depleted populations (which also served as the baseline controls for the 18-library UTY/KDM5D rescue cohort described in Section 3.2.4).

| Cell line | Primary libraries | Model | Genes tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| HT1080 | Six per condition: three matched passage positions in batch 1 and three in batch 2 | $\mathrm{logCPM}_g \sim \mathrm{Batch}+\mathrm{Condition}$ | 25,526 autosomal and non-PAR X-linked genes |
| A549 | Four per condition, collected in one batch | $\mathrm{logCPM}_g \sim \mathrm{RINe}+\mathrm{Condition}$ | 22,843 nuclear genes, including Y-linked and PAR features |
| DU145 | Three per condition, matched at passages P12, P13 and P14 | $\mathrm{logCPM}_g \sim \mathrm{Passage}+\mathrm{Condition}$ | 18,767 autosomal and non-PAR X-linked genes |

Here, $\mathrm{logCPM}_g$ is voom-transformed $\log_2$ CPM, condition identifies sgCTRL or Y-depleted populations, and RINe is the continuous RNA integrity score. HT1080 and DU145 repeatedly sampled one established population per condition; A549 samples were modelled unpaired.

For HT1080, the primary differentially expressed gene set required FDR <0.05 and $|\log_2\text{ fold change}| \ge 1$, and directionally consistent genes additionally required matching effect directions in batch 1 and batch 2. A549 and DU145 significance required FDR <0.05; DU145 additionally defined a reporting subset with $|\log_2\text{ fold change}| \ge 0.5$ and matching directions across all three passage pairs. Gene-set enrichment used fgseaMultilevel against frozen MSigDB 2026.1.Hs collections and competitive testing with CAMERA (inter-gene correlation 0.01), ranking genes by signed moderated $t$ statistics for HT1080 and mean $\log_2$ CPM differences for A549 and DU145. Protein-association networks and pathway clustering used STRING v12.0 (score $\ge 700$), MCODE and Metascape. Extended normalization, PCA, A549 QC correlation and network parameters are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S3.2.2**.

#### 3.2.3.3 HT1080 ATAC-seq and RNA–ATAC integration

One HT1080 sgCTRL and one Y-depleted population were sampled at matched passages 11, 12 and 21 ($3 \times 10^6$ cells per sample), treated with DNase I per the provider protocol, cryopreserved in DMEM/10% DMSO, and processed by Active Motif for nuclei isolation, Tn5 tagmentation and Illumina 51-bp paired-end sequencing. Reads were aligned to GRCh38 no-alt using Bowtie2 v2.5.4, retaining properly paired primary alignments on chromosomes 1–22, X and Y with MAPQ $\ge 30$ and excluding mitochondrial, duplicate (Picard) and hg38 blacklist v2 alignments. Library quality metrics are shown in **Supplementary Methods S3.3.1** and **Supp Fig** 3.3.

Peaks were called per library using MACS3 v3.0.4 (paired-end fragments, effective genome size 2,913,022,398 bp, $q < 0.01$) and merged into 501-bp summit-centred iterative-overlap consensus windows ([Grandi et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41596-022-00692-9)) requiring support in at least two of three libraries in either condition. After excluding Y and PAR regions, edgeR::filterByExpr retained 84,510 consensus regions. TMM-normalized counts were analyzed with edgeR robust quasi-likelihood fitting (condition-only primary model; FDR <0.05 primary, FDR <0.10 exploratory) and edgeR::glmTreat ($|\log_2\text{ fold change}| > 0.5$ interval null). Promoters were annotated as TSS $\pm 2$ kb using TxDb.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg38.knownGene v3.22.0.

Transcription-factor motif enrichment and occupancy were evaluated across 879 JASPAR 2024 vertebrate motifs using HOMER v5.1 (GC- and logCPM-matched background regions), chromVAR v1.32.0 with motifmatchr v1.32.0 (limma condition tests on deviation scores across 84,506 regions), and TOBIAS v0.17.5 footprinting (ATACorrect, ScoreBigwig, BINDetect) on depth-matched pooled fragments.

RNA and ATAC profiles were integrated across 13,300 expressed genes with tested promoters. Associations with curated Polycomb/H3K27me3 target sets were tested using stratified Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel tests matching promoter-changed genes to unchanged controls by baseline accessibility, GC content and CpG composition. Distal differential regions (FDR <0.10) were analyzed with rGREAT v2.12.2 (up to 1-Mb regulatory domains). Covariate-adjusted (~ passage + condition), 10-kb background-bin and csaw v1.44.0 loess normalization sensitivities, together with TSS-coverage decile matching, are described in **Supplementary Methods S3.3.1**.

#### 3.2.3.4 Regional expression and DNA copy-number comparisons

Expressed genes were mapped to T2T-CHM13v2.0 coordinates to compare Y-depleted-minus-sgCTRL RNA $\log_2$ CPM differences with lpWGS $\log_2$ copy-number differences across genomic windows (1-Mb windows for HT1080; 10-Mb windows and gene-level bins for A549 and DU145) and chromosome arms using Pearson correlation. Window definitions and gene-count thresholds are provided in **Supplementary Methods S3.3.2**.

#### 3.2.3.5 Transcription-factor target-expression scores

To evaluate whether DU145 expression changes were consistent with altered activity of candidate EMT regulators (GRHL2 and ZEB1), transcription factor–target gene assignments and signed regulatory weights (+1 for transcriptional activation, −1 for repression) were obtained from the CollecTRI meta-resource (Müller-Dott et al., 2023), which compiles literature-curated, ChIP-seq-supported, and motif-supported regulatory interactions. Using the univariate linear model (ULM) method (Badia-i-Mompel et al., 2022), Y-depleted-minus-sgCTRL mean $\log_2$ CPM differences across all expressed genes were regressed against each regulator's signed CollecTRI target weights (assigning a weight of zero to non-target genes), and the resulting slope $t$ statistic was taken as the target-expression score. Where gene symbols mapped to multiple entries, the entry with the highest sgCTRL mean CPM was retained. Scores were recalculated after removing the *GRHL2* target link (a self-activating link in the GRHL2 regulon and a repressed target in the ZEB1 regulon) to verify that the inferred scores were not driven by *GRHL2* transcript reduction alone. Concordance with the top 200 *GRHL2*-co-expressed genes across 1,719 DepMap 26Q1 cell lines was tested by one-sided Fisher's exact test (**Supplementary Methods S3.3.2**).

#### 3.2.3.6 Cross-line expression and gene-set comparisons

HT1080, A549 and DU145 were compared across 16,165 commonly tested genes using Y-depleted-minus-sgCTRL mean $\log_2$ CPM differences and fgseaMultilevel enrichment ranked on these effects. Genes or gene sets were classified as shared when adjusted $P < 0.05$ in at least two backgrounds with concordant directions in all three, and divergent when adjusted $P < 0.05$ in at least two backgrounds with opposite directions. Three-way overlap of genes with $|\text{mean }\log_2\text{ CPM difference}| \ge 0.5$ was evaluated by within-line permutation tests and exact binomial tests against a directional concordance probability of 0.25. Post hoc expression-abundance sensitivity analyses (CPM $\ge 2$, $\ge 5$ or $\ge 10$) are detailed in **Supplementary Methods S3.3.2**.

#### 3.2.3.7 Reanalysis of external Y-loss and chromosome-loss datasets

To evaluate whether transcriptional programs observed in our engineered models recur in external settings, we reanalyzed 18 external Y-loss or Y-paralog perturbation contrasts (**Section 3.2.3.7**) spanning constitutional sex-chromosome aneuploidy bulk RNA-seq studies ([Zhang et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32071206/); [Ahern et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36161909/); [San Roman et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xgen.2023.100462); [Ahern et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11947172/); [Suntharalingham et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-07699-4)), *UTY*/*UTX* knockout hESCs ([Akiyama et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41906541/)), isogenic A549 LOY clones ([Schlüter et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.02.729627)), murine MB49 Y-loss lines and tumours ([Abdel-Hafiz et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06234-x)), and naturally Y-mosaic scRNA-seq datasets from HT1080 spheroids ([Olson et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41961858/)) and Tahoe-100M ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.20.639398)). In parallel, to distinguish Y-specific effects from general chromosome-loss responses (**Supplementary Methods S3.3.4**), we analyzed 16 contrasts across four public chromosome/arm-loss datasets: RPTEC CRISPR-mediated Yq- and 3p-loss scRNA-seq and bulk clones ([Dahiya et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.10.737056)), ReDACT-mediated trisomy-correction lines for 1q, 7p and 8q ([Girish et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg4521)), and RPE1 monosomy 10+18, 13 and 19p-loss models ([Chunduri et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-25288-x)).

For rank-based comparisons across the 18 external Y-loss contrasts and three thesis models (**Supplementary Methods S3.3.3**), eight focal gene sets were evaluated using rank-based area under the curve (AUC), with effects oriented towards Y absence and empirical $P$ values estimated against 500 size-matched random gene sets. Complete accession numbers, sample filtering criteria and study-by-study statistical models are provided in **Supplementary Methods S3.3.3–S3.3.4** and the [external dataset table](../../tables/chapter3/Table_CH3_external_expression_datasets.md).

### 3.2.4 HT1080 UTY and KDM5D rescue

The HT1080 UTY and KDM5D rescue experiment compared six populations sampled on three matched collection occasions in batch 2: sgCTRL, untransduced Y-depleted, empty-vector, UTY, KDM5D and combined UTY–KDM5D. Empty-vector and single-gene populations were derived by separate lentiviral transductions of the same Y-depleted pool; the combined UTY–KDM5D population was generated sequentially by transducing the established G418-selected UTY population with KDM5D followed by puromycin selection.

#### Lentiviral production, transduction and Western blotting

Lentiviral particles were produced in HEK293T cells by co-transfecting transfer plasmids for UTY (pCDH-UTY-F; Addgene, 40617; [Kim et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1128/MCB.01604-12), gift of Kai Ge), KDM5D (TFORF2768; Addgene, 142159; [Sack et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2018.04.019), gift of Feng Zhang) or empty vector (pLX_HA; Addgene, 178534; [Tchasovnikarova et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abj6375), gift of Paul Lehner) with packaging plasmid psPAX2 (Addgene, 12259; gift of Didier Trono) and envelope plasmid pMD2.G (Addgene, 12260; gift of Didier Trono) using PolyJet (SignaGen Laboratories). Producer cells were treated at 24 h with 1 mM sodium butyrate and 4 mM caffeine, and viral supernatants collected at 48 h were concentrated with PEG-it Virus Precipitation Solution (System Biosciences). HT1080 Y-depleted cells (200,000 per well in six-well plates) were transduced in DMEM containing concentrated lentivirus, 8 µg/mL polybrene and 1 mg/mL Synperonic F108, and selected starting at 48 h with 1,000 µg/mL G418 (UTY), 10 µg/mL puromycin (KDM5D and empty vector), or both antibiotics sequentially (combined UTY–KDM5D).

For Western blotting, RIPA lysates supplemented with protease/phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Cell Signaling Technology, 5872) were sonicated, clarified at $12,000 \times g$ for 20 min at 4 °C, quantified using the DC Protein Assay Kit II (Bio-Rad, 5000112), resolved on 10% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred to 0.45 µm Immobilon-FL PVDF membranes (Sigma-Aldrich, IPFL00005) on a Trans-Blot Turbo Transfer System (Bio-Rad, 1704150). Membranes were blocked in Intercept TBS Blocking Buffer (LI-COR), probed overnight at 4 °C with 1:1,000 primary antibodies against UTY (CST, 48779), KDM5D (CST, 78995) and α-actinin (CST, 69758), incubated with 1:10,000 DyLight 800 goat anti-rabbit IgG (Invitrogen, SA5-10036) and DyLight 680 goat anti-mouse IgG (Invitrogen, 35518) secondary antibodies, and imaged on a LI-COR Odyssey CLx system with Image Studio v6.1.

#### RNA-seq and promoter-class recovery analyses

Autosomal and non-PAR X-linked featureCounts (GENCODE v49; 23,992 filtered genes) were TMM-normalized across the 18 rescue-cohort libraries (batch 2) and analyzed with limma-voom adjusting for group and matched collection occasion across 15 contrasts. Transcriptional recovery was evaluated against a 150-gene discovery LOY signature defined purely from the batch 1 HT1080 sgCTRL and Y-depleted libraries (consistent direction across all three passage pairs, $|\text{mean }\log_2\text{ CPM difference}| \ge 0.5$, and batch 1 limma-voom FDR <0.20, taking the 75 largest effects in each direction). Batch 2 rescue libraries were scored using single-sample rank-based percentile scoring following singscore (Foroutan et al., 2018) across the 23,903-gene discovery background, scaling percent recovery from empty vector (0%) to sgCTRL (100%), alongside directional fry gene-set tests across signature halves and baseline RNA/ATAC promoter-change classes.

To test whether baseline promoter accessibility loss modified rescue responsiveness among 225 down-regulated HT1080 genes (58 with lost promoter peaks at ATAC FDR <0.10 and 167 without), ordinary least squares models with HC3 robust standard errors regressed rescue $\log_2$ expression differences on baseline RNA $\log_2$ fold change, mean expression and promoter-loss status across eight population contrasts, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the eight promoter-loss coefficients. Details of the legacy 100-gene signature and greedy Euclidean control-gene matching are provided in **Supplementary Methods S3.4**.
