# loy_reanalysis_v1 — Y-chromosome loss in DepMap 26Q1

A fresh analysis, run 2026-10-02 from the raw tables in this collection. It does not reuse the calls, matrices or results of the three September analyses (`loy_dependencies_v1`, `y_dosage_vulnerabilities_v1`, `rna_y_vulnerabilities_v1`); those are untouched and appear only in the comparison section of the report.

Start with `report.html` (or `report.pdf`).

## Headline results

| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Y status | 383 Y-negative, 588 Y-positive, 172 set aside, of 1,143 annotated male lines. RNA-led call with DNA support; RNA and DNA agree in 756 of 760 lines where both are definite. |
| Pan-cancer dependencies | 230 Y-negative vs 338 Y-positive lines, 17,205 genes. Four pass FDR 0.05: DDX3X, EIF1AX, ZFX, RPS4X. All four survive adjustment for genome doubling, TP53 and aneuploidy and hold under all nine Y-status definitions. Nothing else passes. |
| Pan-cancer drug sensitivities | None at FDR 0.05 in seven dataset-screens, in a cross-dataset mixed model, or by mechanism or target class. |
| Context-specific interactions | 706,324 gene and 522,569 drug interaction tests over more than 110 contexts. The yield equals what shuffled Y labels give. One lead, ARHGEF2 with CIN, not reproduced by RNAi. |
| What the cancer-type tests could detect (added 2026-10-03) | 14 of 24 lineages, 11 of 58 primary diseases and 7 of 108 subtypes have at least five lines in each Y group. For one gene named in advance, a shift of 0.14 to 0.36 Chronos units inside a cancer type is found 80% of the time. Genome-wide it must reach 0.29 (lung) to 1.23 (soft tissue) with the cancer type analysed alone, and under the screen's own rule 8 of the 30 tested cancer types never find an effect that often. The screen would have called an effect like BRAF in BRAF V600E lines in 15 of the 30 (18 analysed alone). 15 of 25 known vulnerabilities, including WRN in MSI and PRMT5 in MTAP-deleted lines, would be found in none even alone. |
| Other models (added 2026-10-04) | Three public sources, each screened for vulnerabilities that go with Y loss. **Organoids** (Sanger CRISPR screens; 47 Y-negative vs 48 Y-positive, 16,547 genes): DDX3X and RPS4X pass, as expected from the cell lines; ZFX does not, EIF1AX is not in the library, and nothing is specific to colorectal or oesophageal cancer. **RNAi** (307 lines, 240,509 tests over 77 contexts): no context-specific call. **Cell lines with organoids:** the effect detectable genome-wide in colorectal and oesophagogastric cancer falls from about 2.9 SD to about 1.4 SD, and no gene passes there. **Primary AML** (BeatAML2; 254 patients, 15 with a Y-loss fraction of at least 0.5; 95 inhibitors): one passes, selumetinib, which is less active with Y loss (tested in 226 patients, 11 of them with a fraction of at least 0.5); the association weakens once RAS-pathway mutations are in the model. No inhibitor is more active with Y loss. |
| DepMap NextGen (added 2026-10-05) | A third CRISPR source: 147 screened organoid and neurosphere models, 65 male models in the cohort (27 Y-negative vs 38 Y-positive), 18,137 genes. **NextGen alone:** three of the four paired genes replicate (DDX3X -0.90 Chronos units, EIF1AX -0.62, RPS4X -0.79). ZFX (-0.10) has the right sign and is not confirmed: p = 0.046 in the model, 0.051 by permutation. The contrast of EIF1AX and ZFX is smaller than in the cell lines because Y-positive NextGen models are more dependent, as a Cas12a guide that also cuts the Y copy would give. Two other genes pass FDR 0.05 (POLR1G, OCEL1) and neither clears the bar set by 300 shuffles, so no new gene is called. The scan's p-values run small because Y-negative screens are noisier. Nothing is specific to oesophagus/stomach, the one cancer type testable. **Three sources together** (17,159 genes): the four paired genes pass (ZFX on the strength of the cell lines), and two more genes cross FDR 0.05 with small effects, POT1 (0.008) and ARHGAP26 (0.037). Neither is a vulnerability of Y loss (POT1 is less needed with it, ARHGAP26 is not a dependency), and neither stays a hit once NextGen's model carries genome doubling, TP53 and aneuploidy; they are not reported as findings. **Pancreas, pooled across sources** (15 vs 6 models): DDX3X, EIF1AX and ZFX show the Y effect, RPS4X does not; a single gene cannot be called genome-wide there. Oesophagogastric cancer with three sources: still no gene (closest DDX3X, FDR 0.07). |
| Driver status inside cancer types (added 2026-10-06) | The context screen tests each driver alteration across all cancer types; this asks the question inside one cancer type. A gene counts as altered with any of a driver mutation, an amplification or a deep deletion. **What can be tested:** of 2,869 pairs of a gene and a lineage with an altered line, 31 have five altered lines per Y group and 13 also have five unaltered lines per Y group; over lineage, disease and subtype that makes 68 splits in 22 cancer types and 14 genes, 60 of them with fewer than ten lines in the smaller altered cell. **What a split could detect:** one gene named in advance needs a shift of 1.5 SD in the median split (0.7 to 1.9) for the Y effect among altered lines, and about 2.0 SD for a difference by driver status. A search over the whole layer needs several SD: the layer's rank-based test reaches 80% detection in 17 of the 68 splits (3.6 SD in the median of those), the other 51 find even a 10 SD shift in only 11% to 79% of simulations because ranks cap how far a few lines can move, and least squares on Chronos scores, which has no such cap and is not calibrated in cells this small, would need about 4.5 SD. In a search no split can show that a Y effect differs by driver status. **Lines needed:** a shift of 2 SD needs 3.3 times today's lines in the median split and one of 1 SD 9.9 times. How large a known effect counts as depends on the gene: DDX3X's 0.75 Chronos units are 1.9 SD of its own spread (3.5 times the lines) and 4.2 SD of a typical gene's (at most 2 times); for the four paired genes the range is 3.5 to 12 times as variable genes and at most 2 to 5 times as typical ones. Checked on real splits cut down and copied back, these multiples are if anything somewhat low. **The tests:** none of 414,052 tests among altered lines and 146,136 tests of a difference passes the rule fixed in advance; 12 of the 14 distinct tests below 1e-5 are the four paired genes, which are seen as a named family in 161 of 272 tests (40% where the smaller cell has five lines, 94% from ten up). By plain least squares on the same ranks, added after an independent review, 11 tests pass FDR 0.05, all DDX3X or EIF1AX. Shuffled Y status gives nominal results. Sanger organoids (8 splits) and three colorectal splits combined with cell lines: nothing passes the rule. |
| Expression and dependency | Dependency on each X gene tracks expression of its own Y partner (DDX3Y, EIF1AY, ZFY, RPS4Y1), shown with partial-deletion lines. ZNF711 expression buffers ZFX dependency. |
| Expression differences | 164 genes outside chrY at FDR 0.05, led by pseudoautosomal genes, which fall by 0.87 log2 units. |
| Random forests | Genome-wide selection confirms the same four genes and no others; no compound is selected. |

## Reading the numbers

- A negative gene effect means "more dependent in Y-negative lines"; a negative drug effect means "Y-negative lines more sensitive". Every drug metric used is lower-is-more-sensitive, confirmed with BRAF-inhibitor and MDM2-inhibitor controls in each dataset.
- "LOY-associated" means FDR below 0.05 in the lineage-adjusted model. "LOY-specific" means it also survives adjustment for genome doubling, TP53 and aneuploidy.
- Y-negative and Y-positive are the integrated call in `y_status_calls.csv` (`y_call`). The same file holds every alternative definition used in the sensitivity analyses.
- In the power section, a simulated gene counts as "found" when its p-value is below the value at which one more test would pass FDR 0.05 in the stored screen. "Named in advance" means one gene chosen beforehand, at p below 0.05; "alone" means the cancer type analysed on its own, genome-wide; "as run" means the screen's rule (interaction and effect inside the cancer type). These are limits of the design, not observed power, and each probability carries a Monte Carlo error of about 0.02.
- In the section on organoids, RNAi and primary AML, organoid effects are in Chronos-equivalent units (the organoid score divided by 0.73, the typical depletion of a common-essential gene in those screens). The combination of cell lines and organoids pools test statistics, not effect sizes, because the two score on different scales. "Supported by both" means the weaker source also passes a one-sided test; "carried by one source" means it does not.
- In primary AML, Y loss is a fraction between 0 and 1 estimated from RNA, because a patient sample is a mixture of cells; values below 0.29 are set to 0. A positive slope means a higher AUC, that is, a less active inhibitor, with more Y loss.
- A gene or inhibitor seen before the screen was run (RPS4X, DDX3X and CORO1A in organoids) counts as replication, not discovery. Follow-up checks on a hit can only weaken it; one check on the AML hit (RAS-pathway mutations) was added after the hit was seen and is labelled so.
- In the NextGen section, the four paired genes (DDX3X, EIF1AX, ZFX, RPS4X) were named before the screen. For each, "replicated" means the cell-line sign and BH below 0.05 among the four; "not replicated" means not that and significantly smaller than in the cell lines; otherwise the cohort cannot tell. The model's p-values run small in NextGen, so the verdict reported is the rule applied to permutation p-values from a million shuffles (`outcome_permutation`): three replicate, and ZFX is reported as not confirmed, because its smaller size may come from the library. That step was added after the first full run. Any other gene in NextGen alone is a discovery only if it also clears a bar from 300 shuffles of Y status; none does.
- With three sources, a gene is combined over the sources that test it. The label says how many of them support it. POT1 and ARHGAP26 cross FDR 0.05 in that combination with effects below 0.10 Chronos units in the cell lines. `nextgen_combined_hits` shows what becomes of each hit under joint shuffles, with one model left out, and with adjusted models; neither of the two is reported as a finding. That follow-up was designed after the hits were seen.
- The pooled pancreas test compares Y groups within strata (screening library in the cell lines, screen format in NextGen) on ranks within each source, and its p-value counts every arrangement of Y status. With 15 against 6 models its smallest possible p-value is 1.0e-04, so it can test the four named genes and cannot call a single gene genome-wide. The cell lines alone had already been tested inside pancreas (Figure 3b), without a significant result.
- In the section on driver status inside cancer types, a split is a gene in a cancer type with five altered lines per Y group. "Among altered lines" is the Y effect in those lines; "difference by driver status" compares it with the Y effect in the unaltered lines of the same cancer type and needs five lines in all four cells. A shift is "found" when its p-value is below a bar: one gene named in advance (p below 0.05), the split searched alone, or the whole layer (one more discovery among all its tests, p below 1.2e-7). The limits are those of the layer's rank-based test: ranks cap how far a few lines can move, so a small split may not reach 80% at any shift, which says something about the test and not about the lines; least squares on Chronos scores has no such cap and is not calibrated in cells this small. "Lines needed" is the multiple of today's lines at which a shift of a given size is found 80% of the time, from simulations on enlarged copies of each split's design with today's bar. How large a known effect counts as depends on the gene: the same Chronos shift is more SDs of a typical gene than of a paired gene's own, more variable, scores. Copies are a little kinder than real lines, so the multiples are somewhat low.
