**Pooled-population and ddPCR rationale, clarified 17 September 2026:** the author chose pooled Y-depletion models to investigate mixed Y-retaining/Y-negative populations and limit clone-specific effects associated with single-cell isolation and expansion. ddPCR was selected as the primary readout because of published LOY applications and its ability to measure changing Y content in mixed populations. The revised opening presents this rationale, then the assay design and the unchanged mixing-series statistics. Pooling is framed as reducing clone-selection effects, not eliminating all genomic or population-level confounding. No claim that LOY is mosaic in most tumours or that this experiment defines a detection limit was added.

Primary ddPCR precedent checked on 17 September: [Danielsson et al., European Journal of Human Genetics 28, 349–357 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41431-019-0533-z), published online 25 October 2019. The primary article's “LOY from droplet digital PCR (ddPCR) data” section describes a shared primer pair and TaqMan probes targeting the AMELX/AMELY sequence difference; ddPCR was used to quantify mosaic LOY in blood, with matched SNP-array and WGS comparisons. This supports the assay rationale, not wholesale transfer of its reaction protocol or detection limits to the present experiment. Tumour subclonality is already supported by the chapter's Qi et al. (2023) source record; the pooled-model sentence states the author's modelling objective.
